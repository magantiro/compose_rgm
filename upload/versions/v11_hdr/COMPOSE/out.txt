                                                     Abstract
 1            Controllable molecular design requires both a representation of how molecules
 2            can change and a mechanism for steering those edits toward desired outcomes.
 3            Yet molecular generation and optimization are typically trained on fixed endpoint
 4            distributions or task objectives, rather than on a reusable transition process that can
 5            be learned once and redirected as design goals evolve. We introduce COMPOSE
 6            (COntrollable Molecular Process Over Stochastic Edits), a generative framework
 7            that represents molecular design as a stochastic sequence of executable edits be-
 8            tween complete, chemically valid molecules. An exact rewrite system defines legal,
 9            variable-size molecular transitions, while a goal-independent reference process
10            learns probability over them. This underlying process is trained once and held
11            fixed, and task-specific control steers the same molecular dynamics toward dif-
12            ferent objectives. On standard similarity-constrained QED editing on ZINC-250k,
13            the same GuacaMol-trained process transfers without retraining and improves on
14            recent size-adaptive graph diffusion results while using fewer returned trajecto-
15            ries. On lead optimization under molecular feasibility and similarity constraints,
16            COMPOSE improves docking performance over the published state of the art while
17            using sixfold fewer docking-oracle evaluations and maintaining the same feasi-
18            ble coverage. Beyond these external benchmarks, COMPOSE uses downstream
19            reachability to guide multi-step decisions and accomodates retargeting on realized
20            molecular trajectories as objectives and constraints change. These results establish
21            that a molecular transition process can be learned once and reused across datasets,
22            objectives, and interventions without retraining the underlying generator.

23   1    I NTRODUCTION
24   Generative modeling has become a central approach to molecular design, supporting both de novo
25   generation and the optimization of existing molecules across increasingly complex chemical spaces
26   [Gómez-Bombarelli et al., 2018, Jin et al., 2018, Vignac et al., 2023, Lee et al., 2025]. Recent graph-
27   based diffusion, flow, and discrete generative models can learn expressive molecular distributions
28   and increasingly support conditional generation toward desired structural and functional properties
29   [Vignac et al., 2023, Campbell et al., 2024, Lee et al., 2025]. These advances have enabled molecular
30   generation to be substantially more flexible, but the learned object is typically still an endpoint
31   distribution or a generator specialized toward a particular task, rather than a reusable representation
32   of transitions between molecular states. For molecular design, this suggests value in modeling the
33   path between structures, since each transformation changes both the next molecule and the set of
34   molecular states that remain reachable afterward.
35   Molecular editing makes this path dependence explicit as each edit changes the set of transformations
36   available from the resulting molecule. Existing editing and lead-optimization methods already
37   exploit sequential molecular modifications through graph-to-graph generation, reinforcement learning,
38   fragment-based search, evolutionary optimization, and MCMC [Jin et al., 2019, Zhou et al., 2019,
39   Jensen, 2019, Xie et al., 2021, Fu et al., 2021]. However, the consequence of a particular edit can
     Submitted to the [INSERT WORKSHOP HERE] at NeurIPS 2026. Do not distribute.
40   extend beyond the molecule it immediately produces. An atom insertion, bond modification, or ring
41   operation can open or eliminate entire downstream branches of the design space. Molecular editing
42   may therefore benefit from representing not only the value of individual structures, but also which
43   molecular futures remain reachable from each state.
44   Despite this progress, existing approaches capture only parts of a transition-centered approach.
45   Learned generators can increasingly be guided toward desired properties, but their generative trajec-
46   tories are often defined through corrupted, masked, or latent states rather than reusable transitions
47   between complete molecules [Vignac et al., 2023, Campbell et al., 2024, Lee et al., 2025]. Molecular
48   editing and search methods instead operate directly on complete molecules, but their proposal mecha-
49   nisms are typically prescribed by the optimizer and coupled to task-specific scoring [Jensen, 2019,
50   Zhou et al., 2019, Xie et al., 2021]. Recent work further shows that pretrained discrete generators can
51   be redirected toward multiple objectives and constraints through inference-time guidance and control
52   [Chen et al., 2025, 2026].Together, these advances motivate a goal-independent generative process
53   over molecular transitions that can be learned once and controlled as design goals change.
54   Given this, we introduce C OMPOSE (COntrollable Molecular Process Over Stochastic Edits), a
55   generative framework that learns a reusable molecular transition process over complete and chemi-
56   cally valid moelcular graphs and controls it across design objectives (Figure 1). C OMPOSE represents
57   generation as a stochastic sequence of executable edits between complete molecules. At each molecu-
58   lar state, an exact executor defines what is possible by enumerating the state-dependent set of legal,
59   trans-dimensional molecular successors. We then learn a goal-independent reference law Rθ over this
60   same successor set from executable molecular trajectories, capturing which molecular continuations
61   are plausible without conditioning on a downstream design goal. A separate finite-horizon controller
62   determines what edits are purposeful by reweighting molecular continuations according to the value
63   of the futures they make reachable. Because generation and control operate on the same explicit
64   molecular support, the transition process can be learned once and redirected across design objectives
65   without retraining the underlying generator.
66   We summarize our main contributions as follows.
67       • We introduce a stochastic molecular process over exact, executable, trans-dimensional rewrites
68         between complete molecular graphs, with a learned reference law defined on canonical molecular
69         successors.
70       • We formulate molecular design as finite-horizon control of this frozen reference process, allowing
71         edits to be valued by the molecular futures they leave reachable without introducing transitions
72         outside the reference support.
73       • We show that the reference process learns state-dependent transition preferences, transfers across
74         external molecular-design tasks without retraining, and supports future-aware editing, mid-
75         trajectory retargeting, and pathwise constraints.

129   3     E XPERIMENTS
130   Our experiments address three questions. First, we ask whether the frozen reference law learns state-
131   dependent preferences among executable molecular edits. Second, we evaluate whether a reference
132   process learned once on GuacaMol transfers to externally defined molecular-design benchmarks.
133   Finally, we study trajectory-level capabilities that endpoint benchmarks do not capture: using down-
134   stream reachability to guide molecular edits, retargeting realized molecular states when objectives
135   change, and enforcing constraints throughout a trajectory.
136   3.1   C OMPOSE LEARNS STATE - DEPENDENT MOLECULAR EDIT PREFERENCES
137   The rewrite system specifies which molecular edits are executable but not how probability should be
138   distributed among them. On 3,545 held-out transitions with source molecules disjoint from training,
139   we compare Rθ with an empirical-family baseline that preserves the same executable support and
140   canonical successor set while allocating probability according to training-set edit-family frequencies.
141   Rθ reduces canonical-successor NLL from 5.37 to 3.90 nats. A matched decomposition shows that
142   86.7% of this improvement comes from learning which successor is appropriate for the current
143   molecule rather than which edit families are globally common (Table 6). We keep Rθ fixed in all
144   subsequent experiments; results restricted to families with data-backed supervision are reported in
145   Appendix K.1.
146   3.2   C OMPOSE PERFORMS COMPETITIVELY ON EXTERNAL DESIGN TASKS
147   We next evaluate whether the molecular process learned on GuacaMol can be reused without retraining
148   Rθ . We fix the reference process and executable rewrite system and apply COMPOSE to two external
149   benchmarks with different design objectives: similarity-constrained QED editing on ZINC-250k,
150   where molecules can grow or shrink during editing, and protein-specific lead optimization, where
151   molecular properties define feasibility and docking score provides the optimization objective. We
152   only modify the task-specific controller between these settings.

185   3.3   C OMPOSE ENABLES TRAJECTORY- LEVEL CONTROL BEYOND FIXED - OBJECTIVE ENDPOINT
186         OPTIMIZATION

187   Endpoint benchmarks score the molecule returned after optimization but do not necessarily capture
188   how decisions are made or revised along the trajectory. Because every COMPOSE state is a complete
189   and chemically valid molecule, control can use downstream reachability, resume from a realized
190   intermediate after an objective change, or restrict which molecular states may be visited along the
191   generative path.
192   Future reachability changes multi-step edit decisions. An edit that improves immediate similarity
193   can still eliminate every successful continuation within the remaining budget. We test this on 65
194   held-out source–target pairs, each with a certified executable path to the target and sources disjoint
195   from development. The local and future-aware policies share the same frozen Rθ , target, edit budget,
196   and executable successors. They differ only in whether the next edit is selected by immediate target
197   similarity or by the continuations available over the remaining budget. Local selection reaches 26
198   of 65 targets, whereas future-aware control reaches 40, rescuing 14 of the 39 local failures without
199   losing a local success (Table 3). In one deterministic rescue, the locally preferred edit has higher
200   immediate similarity but removes the successful continuation, while a slightly less similar edit
201   preserves a path to the target. Exhaustive look-ahead is not required: using hϕ to prioritize a single
202   successor preserves the 40/65 recovery rate while evaluating 34.6% of the continuations required by
203   all-candidate look-ahead. The comparison is reported in Appendix K.2.
204   Realized molecular states can be re-targeted after an objective change. Starting from x0 , we
205   execute three edits under an initial objective and then switch objectives at the realized state x3 . We
206   compare continuing from x3 with restarting from x0 , holding the new objective and remaining edit
207   budget fixed. Across 40 held-out molecules, continuing from x3 improves post-switch utility in
208   both directions: 0.302 [0.186, 0.413] for the potency-first objective and 0.176 [0.069, 0.283] for the
209   developability-first objective (Table 3). The continued trajectories also move toward the new objective
210   rather than preserving the preference under which x3 was reached. Retargeting requires no update to
211   Rθ ; control resumes from the realized molecular state under the new objective.
212   Pathwise constraints act on the molecular trajectory. Some optimization settings may necessitate
213   molecular requirements to hold at every intermediate rather than only for the returned endpoint.
214   COMPOSE enforces such statewise constraints by removing violating successors before the reference
215   kernel is normalized. We compare this support restriction with endpoint-only filtering under matched
216   sources, objectives, and edit budgets. On the developmental panel, 16 of 24 endpoint-feasible
217   trajectories contained at least one intermediate constraint violation, showing that endpoint feasibility
218   can hide inadmissible paths. Support-restricted control excludes such intermediates by construction.
219   We report the matched effect on optimization performance and the full protocol in Appendix K.7.

220   4     D ISCUSSION
221   COMPOSE is built around a reference process over chemically valid molecules that is learned
222   independently of any downstream objective. In our experiments, the same GuacaMol-trained Rθ is
223   reused for variable-size editing on ZINC-250k and for protein-specific lead optimization, with only
224   the controller changing between tasks. Representing the intermediate states explicitly as molecules
225   also enables the model to look ahead before committing an edit, continue from a molecule already

228   Nevertheless the current formulation still has several limitations: finite-horizon control requires
229   evaluating the downstream states reachable within the remaining edit budget, which is only feasible
230   on small enumerable state graphs. For realistic molecular design problems, we instead learn hϕ to
231   approximate this future value from sampled continuations. COMPOSE is also limited by its rewrite
232   vocabulary and by the property models or docking scores used to define downstream objectives.
233   Extending the executable chemistry and evaluating against experimentally measured objectives are
234   natural next steps.

296   A     R ELATED WORK
297   Molecular generation spans latent-variable, graph-to-graph, reinforcement-learning, diffusion, and
298   flow-based approaches, while molecular optimization has also been formulated directly through
299   graph edits, fragment proposals, evolutionary operators, and MCMC transitions [Gómez-Bombarelli
300   et al., 2018, Jin et al., 2018, 2019, Zhou et al., 2019, Jensen, 2019, Xie et al., 2021, Fu et al., 2021,
301   Vignac et al., 2023, Campbell et al., 2024]. Recent work has begun to relax the fixed-size assumption
302   of graph generation: GrIDDD introduces node insertion and deletion into discrete graph diffusion,
303   allowing molecular graphs to grow and shrink during denoising [Ninniri et al., 2025]. C OMPOSE
304   instead learns a goal-independent stochastic law over an explicit, state-dependent molecular rewrite
305   space, making the transition process itself the object of subsequent control.
306   A related line of work treats generation and adaptation directly through stochastic processes. Generator
307   matching provides a general framework for learning Markov generators from tractable conditional
308   processes, while Edit Flows develops insertion, deletion, and substitution dynamics for variable-length
309   discrete generation [Holderrieth et al., 2025, Havasi et al., 2025]. MadSBM is a close mathematical
310   relative, formulating peptide generation as a controlled Markov process on an amino-acid edit graph,
311   although its learned control constructs the generative transport rather than adapting an already learned
312   molecular process [Goel and Chatterjee, 2026]. Value Matching steers a frozen flow model through
313   a learned value function, and pCoMole studies Pareto-constrained molecular editing with discrete
314   flows [Jensen et al., 2026, Chen et al., 2026]. Future-aware generative control is therefore not unique
315   to C OMPOSE: value-guided flow adaptation and pCoMole likewise use learned or rollout-estimated
316   future value. Our distinction is the substrate on which that control operates. It is a learned canonical
317   transition law over executable molecular graphs, which is what allows realized molecular states to be
318   reused, retargeted, and constrained.
319   Multi-objective molecular design has been addressed through surrogate-based optimization, evolu-
320   tionary search, and inference-time guidance of generative models. InversionGNN combines multi-
321   property prediction with gradient-based Pareto search in discrete molecular space [Niu et al., 2025],
322   while PepTune and AReUReDi use discrete generative models with multi-objective tree search or
323   Pareto-oriented refinement [Tang et al., 2025, Chen et al., 2025]. Routing by Reaching similarly
324   composes pretrained GFlowNets for multi-objective generation at inference time [Yoon et al., 2026].
325   C OMPOSE instead places multi-objective control on a reusable molecular transition process, so that
326   future reachability and already-reached molecular states can participate directly in Pareto exploration.

347   C     T HE EXECUTABLE MOLECULAR PROCESS IN FULL
348   We develop C OMPOSE as a reusable stochastic process over molecular edits and a finite-horizon
349   controller over that process. C OMPOSE realizes generative stochastic rewriting by repeatedly applying
350   executable molecular edits under a learned, goal-independent transition law. An exact rewrite system
351   first determines which transitions between complete molecular graphs are executable. We then
352   learn a goal-independent reference law over this state-dependent support and push it forward to a
353   canonical molecular successor kernel Rθ . Finally, we control the frozen successor process through a
354   budget-conditioned h-transform, using a learned future-value model hϕ to approximate the required
355   backward values at molecular scale.

555   D.5    R ETARGETING AND PATHWISE MOLECULAR CONSTRAINTS
556   Because control is conditioned on the current molecular state, C OMPOSE can change either the
557   design objective or the admissible transition set without retraining the molecular reference process.
558   Suppose a trajectory controlled under objective z reaches the realized molecule xτ with b transitions
559   remaining, after which the objective changes to z ′ . Control resumes by evaluating the same kernel in
560   Equation (23) from xτ under the new goal context z ′ . The realized prefix x0 → · · · → xτ is retained,
561   and no parameter of Rθ is updated.
562   Corollary 4 (Exact continuation after a goal change). With exact backward values, applying The-
563   orem 3 from (xτ , b) with terminal desirability gz′ realizes the gz′ -tilted b-step terminal law of the
564   reference process started at xτ . This generally differs from specifying z ′ at the original source x0 :
565   continuation preserves the molecular state actually reached, whereas restarting discards it. With
566   learned hϕ , the same operation gives the corresponding approximate continuation.
567   Hard statewise requirements can instead be enforced through executable support. Let C : X → {0, 1}
568   be a molecular predicate with C(x0 ) = 1, and define the restricted edit set
                                            
                                  AC (x) = a ∈ A(x) : C(T(x, a)) = 1 .                              (28)
569   Applying the canonicalization and normalization of Appendix C.3 to AC (x) gives the constrained
570   reference kernel RθC . If AC (x) = ∅, the state is terminal under the constrained process; otherwise
571   every committed successor satisfies C by construction. Finite-horizon control then proceeds with RθC
572   in place of Rθ . Approximation error in hϕ may therefore misallocate probability among admissible
573   successors, but cannot violate a constraint enforced through the support itself.
574   This construction applies to state predicates whose admissibility can be determined from the proposed
575   molecular successor; we refer to such constraints as rule-closed. Examples include protected scaffolds,
576   forbidden substructures, and size or charge restrictions. Requirements that depend on accumulated
577   history require an augmented state. For a sufficient statistic ξk , define
                                   X
                                   ek = (Xk , ξk ),       ξk+1 = Φ(ξk , Xk+1 ),
578   so that the augmented process remains Markov. Cumulative edit costs or other history-dependent
579   requirements can then be represented explicitly rather than imposed through a non-Markov rejection
580   rule.
581   Retargeting and pathwise restriction therefore modify different parts of the same frozen process:
582   retargeting changes the control context, whereas hard constraints change executable support. In both
583   cases, the parameters of the learned molecular reference process remain fixed.

596   rather than post-hoc sanitization. This section specifies that state space and the rewrite contracts that
597   induce its transition graph. The semantic state is the active molecular graph on which connectivity,
598   valence, ring perception, and canonical identity are evaluated, and we distinguish it from any padded
599   tensor representation used by the implementation. We then define the current operator family, its exact
600   legality checks, and the pushforward from executable rewrite marks to canonical molecular successors.
601   These definitions determine what transitions are possible before either Rθ or a task-specific controller
602   assigns probability to them.


                                                        15
603   F.1   M OLECULAR STATE SPACE
604   States carry a bounded number of heavy atoms over a declared set of neutral (element, valence)
605   classes drawn from an organic element set; the declared bounds and vocabulary are fixed in the frozen
606   process configuration and are identical across every experiment reported here. Formal charge is an
607   input but not an output dimension, so charge is carried rather than designed; stereochemistry, isotopes
608   and radicals are not represented.
609   Cardinality is a property of the active molecular graph rather than of a fixed tensor allocation. Writing
610   Xn for the states with n heavy atoms, the state space is the disjoint union of the strata, and a
611   birth or death moves a trajectory between strata by creating or destroying a typed graph entity that
612   participates in valence, connectivity, and ring perception. This is not the activation or deactivation of
613   a padded coordinate, and the distinction is what makes the process trans-dimensional in the sense
614   used throughout the paper.

632   F.3   C OMPILED MULTI - EDIT TRANSFORMATIONS
633   The lead-optimization controller may select a multi-edit ring transformation, such as constructing
634   or refining a ring system, rather than a single primitive edit. These transformations are compiled
635   programs, not new operators. A program of length L executes L primitive edits in sequence; at every
636   step the successor is drawn from the same legal-successor enumeration A(·) used everywhere else,
637   applied through the same executor, and screened by the same guards. A program halts if a state admits
638   no legal successor.
639   Two consequences matter for the claims in this paper. First, the executable support is unchanged:
640   every endpoint a program can reach is reachable by the corresponding sequence of primitive rewrites,
641   so compiling ring construction introduces no molecular successor that A(·) does not already admit,
642   and Appendix G therefore applies unchanged to the controlled process. Second, what a program
643   changes is the granularity of control, not the support: purpose is evaluated at the program endpoint
644   rather than after each edit, because a constructive ring program passes through intermediate states
645   whose immediate objective value is worse than both its start and its endpoint. Scoring each edit
646   separately prunes such a program before it completes, which is a property of the control schedule and
647   not of the state space.

649   Distinct executable marks can produce the same molecule, so the law the controller acts on is the

651   (15)–(17). Aggregation is what makes every downstream quantity independent of how many ways
652   the implementation can name a molecule; Appendix G.1 establishes the precise invariance this buys
653   and its exact scope.

771   H.1    M OLECULAR DATA AND PREPROCESSING
772   The editing corpus was derived from a fixed 500,000-molecule GuacaMol source pool by compiling
773   executable local transitions, real-endpoint analogue relations, and reversible synthetic walks. C OM -
774   POSE was not trained as an unconditional distribution model over that pool: the learned object is
775   Rθ (y | x), a transition law over source–successor edits, not a density pθ (x) over molecules.

777   corroborated by the molecular statistics of the frozen training sources. Measured on 4,000 randomly

783   the original raw asset end to end, we treat this provenance link as strongly supported rather than

881   spread of true values across candidate successors shrinks, so target reliability degrades with horizon.
882   Appendix K.5 reports that degradation and the rollout counts it implies.
883   Because supervision quality is horizon-dependent, learned future-value control is admitted only at
884   horizons whose target reliability meets a criterion fixed in advance of any optimization result. The
885   admitted horizon and its rollout count are recorded in the frozen controller configuration, and the
886   deployed score is hϕ (y, λ, b − 1) for b ≤ bFA and gλ (y) otherwise.

887   J     S AMPLING AND CONTROL
888   We use two numerical realizations of molecular control. Direct or rejection-based sampling is used
889   when the controlled row can be evaluated efficiently, whereas low-reference-mass objectives are
890   handled with the sequential Monte Carlo construction below. Both retain the frozen molecular
891   reference process and differ only in how the controlled distribution is realized computationally.

892   J.1   E XACT FINITE - HORIZON CONTROL
893   Where the set of molecular states reachable within the remaining budget can be enumerated, the
894   backward value of (18) is computed by exact dynamic programming: initialize h0 = gz on the
895   enumerated terminal layer and sweep backwards, at each budget taking the expectation of the next
896   layer’s value under Rθ restricted to the legal edit set. The controlled kernel of (19) follows by
897   division and Theorem 3 applies without approximation. Accordingly, empirical claims of exact
898   finite-horizon control refer only to enumerable settings in which the backward values are computed
899   without approximation.

900   J.2   M OLECULAR - SCALE SAMPLING
901   At scale hϕ replaces the exact value. Because it is a state-level function taking values in [0, 1], the
902   controlled row admits a rejection realization: propose Y ∼ Rθ (· | x), execute, canonicalize, reject
903   self-transitions, and accept with probability hϕ (Y, z, b − 1). Conditioned on acceptance the move has
904   exactly the Rθ · hϕ law, and aliased successors aggregate correctly because the acceptance probability
905   depends only on the resulting molecule. The proposal cap is finite, however, so when every proposal
906   at a state is rejected the trajectory ends; the capped procedure is therefore not an exact realization
907   of the controlled kernel and is not described as one. Cap pressure is recorded as a diagnostic with a
908   preregistered escalation to the sequential realization below, which changes inference only.
909   When the desired region carries little mass under the reference process, control is realized as a twisted
910   Feynman–Kac particle system. Every particle proposal comes from the frozen reference law alone,
911   and hϕ enters only through the incremental weight (24). Using the value to both select and weight
912   would double-count control and sample a different process. The weight product telescopes along a
913   trajectory to h0 (xH )/hH (x0 ), and the terminal boundary h0 (x) = 1[x ∈ Bz ] is exact, so a learned
914   twist changes particle allocation without changing the terminal success criterion. The operating
915   point is frozen and no particle-count sweep is permitted: N = 32 particles, horizon 24, absorbing
916   stop on entryPto the goal region, and systematic resampling whenever the effective sample size
917   ESS(w) = ( i wi2 )−1 on normalized weights falls strictly below N/2. Resampling is systematic
918   rather than multinomial, a single uniform draw positioning the whole comb. Per-transition values,
919   per-step resampling decisions and resampling indices are recorded so the mechanical checks run
920   offline against the artifact.
921   Candidate accounting follows one rule: one run returns one molecule. A source’s twenty candidates are
922   twenty independent runs, each contributing the single molecule drawn from its terminal normalized
923   particle measure. The particles within a run are inference state and are never counted as candidates,
924   and intermediate states are never retrospectively harvested. A run reaching the budget without entering
925   the region is recorded under a frozen extinction status rather than replaced. For a region goal the
926   controller stops at the first committed molecule inside the region, an online stopping time evaluated
927   on the realized state.

929   Under a preference λ the immediate arm weights successors by gλ (y) and the future-aware arm by

1139   prespecified sensitivity analysis. The panel is sized on the joint criterion, giving power 0.976 at the
1140   0.25 margin and 0.874 at 0.20. No held-out source has been opened.
1141   A value head retrained to be aware of a longer horizon was built, evaluated and rejected on four
1142   separate measurements, and is not the head used anywhere in Appendix K. The consequence for the
1143   main text is a claim boundary: any gain from a longer horizon comes from giving the frozen process
1144   more edit opportunities, not from a better budget-conditioned value model.

1146   L.1    QED EDITING BENCHMARK
1147   We adopt the official 800-source cohort, property and similarity criteria, and evaluator from the
1148   benchmark, while evaluating C OMPOSE with K = 8 rather than the published K = 20 allowance:
1149   the 800 test molecules with QED ∈ [0.7, 0.8] as sources, 20 candidates per source, a Tanimoto
1150   similarity constraint of 0.4 to the source, and success defined as at least one returned candidate
1151   reaching QED ∈ [0.9, 1.0] while satisfying that constraint. Source selection, fingerprints, property
1152   calculations, candidate count and aggregation must be identical for the comparison to stand, and
1153   where any of these could not be matched it is stated rather than assumed.
1154   Output contract. Each source receives exactly K candidate slots. A trajectory that reaches the
1155   target region returns the first committed molecule satisfying the benchmark criterion. If no sampled
1156   continuation reaches the region, the slot returns the unchanged canonical source solely to preserve the
1157   fixed-K benchmark interface; this output necessarily fails because benchmark sources begin below
1158   the target QED threshold. No failed slot is resampled or replaced. This contract is frozen before the
1159   official cohort is opened and is not revised afterwards.
1160   Every non-source returned molecule is a benchmark success: each slot either returns a molecule in the
1161   target region or returns the unmodified source. A smaller candidate allowance can disadvantage both
1162   success and the diversity metric, because a source with only one successful distinct return contributes
1163   zero diversity. Separately, no returned success is identical to its source, so the count is unchanged
1164   under the stricter rule of the released success script, which requires similarity strictly below one.
1165   Published values are reproduced as reported and are not re-derived. Because the comparator does not
1166   fully specify the evaluator used for its main diversity entry, we do not interpret the diversity values as
1167   an exactly matched comparison. Among published entries for this task, the GrIDDD row at 45.1% is
1168   the one we compare against. Higher rates have been reported on this task outside the comparator’s
1169   table, and Appendix L.3 records them so that Table 10 is not read as the complete published record.
1170   Diversity is computed with the released evaluation script of Jin et al. [2019] rather than reimplemented
1171   from its prose description. That script admits a candidate only if it satisfies both the similarity


                                                              29
1172   constraint and the property target, deduplicates the admitted molecules, excludes a source with
1173   no admitted molecule from the average, and assigns a diversity of exactly zero to a source with
1174   only one. Similarity is a Morgan fingerprint of radius two on 2048 bits with chirality disabled. The
1175   convention matters. Of the 446 solved sources on the official panel, 335 return at least two distinct
1176   non-source molecules and average 0.4999; the remaining 111 return a single distinct molecule and
1177   enter the released script’s average as zero, giving 0.376 over all solved sources. We report the 0.4999
1178   figure and state its denominator wherever it appears. C OMPOSE returns on average 3.63 distinct
1179   successful molecules of the eight permitted, and no successful candidate is identical to its source, so
1180   the success count is unchanged under the stricter rule of the released script, which requires similarity
1181   strictly below one. All returned strings are canonical, so the string-level deduplication of the released
1182   script coincides with deduplication by molecule, and no pair of distinct returned molecules shares a
1183   fingerprint under the achiral setting.
1184   GrIDDD does not specify the evaluator used for the diversity entry in its main property-optimization
1185   table. Ninniri et al. [2025] do not state how the diversity column of their main property-optimization
1186   table is computed. Their baseline diversity values are identical to those reported in the graph-to-graph
1187   translation line, which places the inherited rows on the scale defined above, but the method’s own
1188   entry is undocumented; a rule matching the one used here is stated only in their appendix, for a
1189   separately adapted protocol with a different number of optimization rounds. We therefore report
1190   our value as computed under a named and audited definition, and we do not assert that it and the
1191   published entry were produced by the same procedure.
1192   Because every C OMPOSE trajectory terminates prospectively on entering the goal region, a run either
1193   returns a molecule satisfying both constraints or goes extinct, and the set the diversity script admits is
1194   exactly the set of successful returns. The admitted set is therefore constructed the same way as in the
1195   published rows rather than merely being similar to it. Both now use the official 800 sources, and the
1196   residual difference is the candidate allowance, eight against twenty. That difference is not neutral for
1197   diversity: a smaller allowance yields fewer second successes per source and so more single-molecule
1198   sources contributing zero, which depresses the reported value relative to what the same system would
1199   produce at twenty candidates.
1200   The benchmark constrains returned molecules and says nothing about internal successor evaluations,
1201   and competing methods use their native inference procedures. The comparison therefore measures
1202   task performance under a fixed output allowance, no efficiency claim is made, and inference ledgers
1203   are reported separately. The printed entry is the official 800-source cohort at eight of the twenty
1204   permitted candidates, so it uses fewer outputs than the benchmark allows.
1205   L.2   G EN M OL T4 LEAD - OPTIMIZATION BENCHMARK
1206   The protocol is adopted from Lee et al. [2025]: five protein targets, three seed molecules per target,
1207   and similarity thresholds δ ∈ {0.4, 0.6}, with a returned molecule required to satisfy QED ≥ 0.6,
1208   SA ≤ 4, and Tanimoto similarity at least δ to the seed. The reported statistic in the comparator’s table
1209   is the mean docking score of the most optimized lead over three runs. The COMPOSE entry for a
1210   cell is the best feasible docking score obtained within that cell’s 500-call budget.
1211   Two protocol details govern how the comparison is read. First, the constraints are endpoint con-
1212   straints: neither method is required to keep intermediate states feasible, so the comparison is about
1213   returned molecules only. Second, oracle budget is counted in docking calls. The comparator’s entry
1214   corresponds to 1,000 calls per run over three runs; the COMPOSE entry consumes 500 calls for the
1215   cell. Inexpensive property evaluations are excluded from the count on both sides.
1216   Dashes in the published table mark cells for which no feasible molecule is reported. We carry them
1217   through as missing rather than as zeros, report coverage separately from score, and restrict the paired
1218   comparison to cells where both methods report a feasible molecule. Published values for GenMol,
1219   RetMol and Graph GA are reproduced as reported and are not re-derived.

1242   M.2     R ANDOMIZATION AND STATISTICS
1243   Run seeds are derived rather than drawn, by hashing a protocol tag together with the source molecule
1244   and the replicate index and taking the leading eight bytes of the digest. In paired comparisons the
1245   arm name is deliberately excluded from the seed, so arms sharing a source and replicate index
1246   consume identical randomness and a difference between them cannot be a seeding artifact. Where
1247   an arm-specific seed is unavoidable for later replicates, the first replicate is still constructed to be
1248   identical across arms.
1249   Intervals are bootstrap intervals resampled over the independent unit named in each experiment. That
1250   unit is the source molecule in most cases, the source–target pair in the sealed lookahead panel, and
1251   the state in the reliability analysis of Appendix K.5. Replicate counts differ by analysis, ranging
1252   from 4,000 to 8,192 draws, and each artifact records its own. Where an interval is exact rather than
1253   bootstrapped, that is stated with the reason.

1265   M.4    T HE MEDICINAL - CHEMISTRY VALIDITY GATE
1266   The gate rejects a returned molecule for a small, explicitly enumerated set of reasons: elements
1267   outside {C,N,O,F,S,Cl,Br,I,P}; a halogen carrying hydrogens or more than one heavy neighbour
1268   (hypervalent iodine such as [IH4]); sulfur carrying hydrogens with two or more heavy neighbours;
1269   phosphorus that is neither phosphate nor phosphonate; radicals; formal charge beyond ±1; a ring
1270   larger than seven whose atoms are mostly not shared with another ring; a nitrogen–nitrogen bond
1271   inside a three- or four-membered ring; and cumulated double bonds.
1272   Two of these conditions were narrowed after calibration refuted a broader version. A flat ring-size
1273   cutoff was rejected because the benchmark seeds themselves report 7- and 8-membered SSSR
1274   envelope rings over fused tricyclic cores, so ring size alone cannot separate a fused drug scaffold
1275   from a macrocycle; the condition now applies only to rings whose atoms are largely unshared. A
1276   minimum-ring-size condition for pendant systems was rejected because a published InVirtuoGen
1277   winner carries a cyclopropane; that minimum was moved out of the validity gate and into the definition
1278   of the specific construction operation it belongs to.
1279   The calibration is a test rather than a claim: the gate must accept all fifteen benchmark seeds and
1280   all twenty-five published InVirtuoGen winners, and reject the implausible molecules. The suite
1281   also asserts that ordinary sulfur chemistry (thiophene, thioether, sulfone) still passes, which is what
1282   prevents the hypervalent-sulfur condition from over-rejecting.
1283   We distinguish two gates. A pathwise gate asks only whether a state is an executable molecule and
1284   is applied to every intermediate; the full gate, which additionally imposes QED, SA and similarity,
1285   is applied only to returned molecules. Conflating them is not conservative: applying the full gate to
1286   every intermediate prevents a trajectory from passing through a temporarily unattractive molecule,
1287   and measurably degraded search.

1288   M.5    G ENERALIZATION OF A SINGLE - CELL CONTROLLER
1289   The lead-optimization controller described in Table 1 was developed against one cell. Applied
1290   unchanged to three further cells it returned no constraint-satisfying molecule from three hundred
1291   trajectories each. The failure is quantitative rather than mysterious: the similarity budget is a property
1292   of the seed, and the seed the controller was tuned on carries 41 Morgan-fingerprint bits while another
1293   carries 29, so an edit program calibrated on the first spends a much larger fraction of the second.
1294   Replacing the fixed program with an adaptive search over the same action vocabulary — identical
1295   operators, identical reference law, no new chemistry — recovered feasibility on all three cells. We
1296   report this as a limitation of fixed edit programs rather than as a positive result, since the recovered
1297   molecules were not competitive; but it does locate the failure in the controller’s lack of adaptation
1298   rather than in the operator set or the reference law.
