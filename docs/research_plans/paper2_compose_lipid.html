Thesis & novelty — same story, sharper ownership

Working thesis

COMPOSE-Lipid adapts Rewrite Generator Matching to ionizable-lipid chemistry, generates complete molecules through chemistry-guarded stochastic rewrites rather than ranking a fixed library, and prospectively tests whether generated candidates improve the in-vivo lung-delivery hit rate under a matched experimental budget.

The distinctive claim remains exactly learned de-novo generation plus prospective in-vivo validation: to our knowledge, the first learned de-novo ionizable-lipid generative model whose model-sampled candidates—rather than candidates selected from a pre-enumerated virtual library—are prospectively synthesized, formulated, and validated in vivo. LUMI-lab and MOLEA are important AI-plus-in-vivo comparators, but their reported discovery engines are predictive foundation-model/active-learning and multiobjective prediction/optimization systems, respectively—not learned molecular generators that sample new lipid structures. Conversely, LiGen and the synthesis-aware lipid models are genuine generative precedents, but they do not report prospective in-vivo validation of model-generated candidates. The “first” remains explicitly qualified and is locked only after a dated, model-by-model prior-art audit at submission.

Author-team lineage · citation, not a dependency

Our GEM 2026 workshop study, Synthesis-constrained discrete diffusion for ionizable lipid generation, is related prior work and will be cited transparently. COMPOSE-Lipid is nevertheless built independently from the COMPOSE RGM engine and its own audited corpus: it reuses no GEM data, preprocessing, code, model components, or checkpoints, and no matched GEM reproduction is required for activation. The scientific delta is established directly through flexible-size topology-changing generation, validity-closed rewrites, reaction-diverse coverage, conditional control, and prospective experimental validation.

What the paper is—and is not—claiming

The paper does not claim the first use of AI, active learning, combinatorial design, molecular generation in silico, or in-vivo lipid testing separately. Subject to the locked prior-art audit, it claims the previously missing conjunction: a learned de-novo ionizable-lipid generator whose own sampled candidates cross the full prospective chain through synthesis, formulation, and in-vivo validation. RGM supplies the imported generative engine and an additional methodological distinction; the controlled selection-versus-guidance comparison and linker-constrained transfer strengthen the paper but do not replace that central claim.

How the two papers share one engine

Paper 1 (COMPOSE, ICLR) introduces and validates the structured molecular RGM method. Stochastic rewriting defines typed rules, concrete matches, application conditions, inverses, and the legal molecular state graph. Certified endpoint-conditioned rewrite programs define conditional CTMC generators; inherited GM/LPGM identities yield the target-free marginal process, while COMPOSE contributes the chemically admissible bridge and learned rewrite support. The model sees only the current molecule and time at inference and samples an ancestral CTMC with no compiler, endpoint, beam, or repair. Paper 2 proves the impact by adapting this interface to lipid chemistry and prospective biology.

Logical dependency and chosen execution order

Paper 2 does not depend on Paper 1 being submitted, accepted, or published. Lipid source acquisition, provenance, corpus auditing, and the pan-lung endpoint schema can proceed now. We nevertheless choose a risk-controlled model handoff: first freeze a credible COMPOSE unconditional generator, qualify construct-plus-recovery behavior, and clear one matched ZINC-QED conditional/optimization proof; then activate lipid kernel training, generator training, and the frozen lung-oracle campaign. The remainder of Paper 1 can continue after that handoff, while prospective candidates still require a frozen, versioned, lipid-ready core.

02

The one scientific move to add: two generation strategies, head-to-head

Your draft selects candidates one way — an unconditional generator scored by a frozen oracle. The conditional positioning gives you a second, and testing both in vivo at matched budget addresses a question not reported in the audited lipid-generation literature.

Arm A · required workhorse

Base RGM + frozen lung oracle

Sample target-free ancestral trajectories from the frozen lipid generator; rank with a deduplicated multi-study oracle that shares representations across pulmonary evidence while preserving study-, route-, cell-, and readout-specific heads, plus its locked uncertainty/applicability-domain rule, synthesis filters, and diversity-aware Pareto selection.

Arm B · gated upside

Lung-guided, validity-preserving RGM

Start from the identical base checkpoint and use the same frozen multi-study lung oracle to tilt only legal successor rates—or fine-tune the marked CTMC under a KL/trajectory anchor. Guidance optimizes only the calibrated head matching the locked prospective route and readout; it changes probabilities but never enables an illegal rewrite. Out-of-distribution improvement is a hypothesis, not a promised result.

Run both, plus matched non-AI controls (random/DoE and expert/rational), on one blinded panel if power and synthesis budget allow. The primary contrast is AI-selected versus matched non-AI hit-rate enrichment. Arm A versus Arm B is prespecified secondary unless the power analysis supports a co-primary claim.

Why running both is the safe and ambitious choice

Arm A is the required translational path. Arm B is included only after the oracle and guidance gates pass; if it exploits uncertainty, collapses diversity, or leaves the applicability domain, it becomes a computational negative result or a round-2-only method. The paper never depends on Arm B beating Arm A.

03

What Rewrite Generator Matching adds to the lipid campaign

RGM makes the intervention space explicit. Linker or scaffold preservation is an application condition that removes violating rewrites from the legal fiber, not a soft reward that may be broken. Grow/shrink, restatement, bond revision, Graft, and whole-ring-system events let the model correct size and topology rather than only append fragments. Every accepted intermediate is a complete connected graph, so a property model can score the trajectory at any event.

Those are algorithmic affordances, not biological guarantees. They do not make the oracle accurate, certify synthesis, or predict in-vivo success. Round-2 lead optimization is therefore a clean, testable capability hypothesis: begin from a measured lead, freeze the novel linker, and learn or guide short valid recovery trajectories around it.

The concrete implementation dependency

Local lead optimization requires the recovery/revision objective to produce adequate inverse-operator supervision. The forward carbon-tree-to-data teacher alone does not establish learned correction. Before Figure 6 candidates are locked, the model must pass source-conditional editing gates for shrink, Graft reversal, ring-system deletion, scaffold preservation, similarity, and diversity.

What RGM adds without changing the translational story

  • Exact stochastic-rewrite substrate: rules, legal matches, application conditions, inverses, and canonical successor aggregation define the visible molecular state graph.
  • GM/LPGM inheritance and COMPOSE bridge: certified endpoint programs teach contextual CTMC rates; generic GM/LPGM identities project away the target and latent program, while COMPOSE supplies the chemically admissible bridge and learned rewrite support.
  • Pathwise validity and connectivity: guidance can change rates only within the same hard legal action fiber.
  • Hard linker/scaffold conditions: the round-2 linker is protected structurally rather than encouraged through a soft penalty.
  • One process for de novo and editing: the base generator and recovery-trained source-conditional editor share rewrite semantics and rate parameterization.
  • Central novelty: to our knowledge, the first learned de-novo ionizable-lipid generator taken prospectively through synthesis, formulation, and in-vivo validation; RGM, the controlled strategy comparison, and linker-constrained transfer make that claim more technically and experimentally distinctive.
  • The original evidence chain remains: two AI arms, matched controls, preregistration, biodistribution, tolerability, independent replication, functional cargo, and linker-specific round-2 SAR.

The principal computational risk

Arm B inherits the oracle's blind spots and may maximize uncertainty rather than delivery. Pre-register the applicability-domain and abstention rules, preserve Arm A as the required workhorse, and treat failure of guided generation as a measured result rather than a reason to compromise the prospective design.

Primary landscape anchors

  • LUMI-lab — predictive foundation model plus active learning over an enumerable reaction-defined space, with synthesis and in-vivo lung editing; an AI-plus-in-vivo comparator, not a learned de-novo molecular generator.
  • MOLEA — learned lipid representations and multiobjective prediction/optimization over a virtual library, with in-vivo selectivity and functional editing; an AI-plus-in-vivo comparator, not a reported generative molecular sampler.
  • LiGen — genuine predictor-guided lipid language-model generation without prospective synthesis or in-vivo validation.
  • Maganti, Maganti & Alameh, Synthesis-constrained discrete diffusion for ionizable lipid generation (GEM 2026) — related fixed-size/fixed-topology Ugi-scaffold work from our group, cited transparently but not reused or required as an internal baseline.
  • A Deep Generative Model for the Design of Synthesizable Ionizable Lipids — genuine synthesis-path generation and computational optimization; no prospective wet-lab study reported.
  • Wang et al. — SHAP-informed head/tail combinatorial library construction plus predictive virtual screening and in-vivo validation; not a learned de-novo generator.
  • LNPDB — 19,528 formulations, 12,845 unique ionizable lipids, and heterogeneous experimental metadata.
  • COMET/LANCE — transformer-based LNP design with experimental and in-vivo validation.
