The thesis under test

What the whole program is arguing

One learned molecular process, frozen, supports exact target recovery, mid-trajectory goal change, and pathwise structural constraints through different control operations — with no retraining between them.

The layers, in order: legality (the rewrite executor defines what is executable) → plausibility (Rθ learns which molecular continuation is likely) → purpose (finite-horizon control picks which plausible continuation is useful). All four layers now have evidence. The fourth — intervening on a trajectory already underway — is the result reported below; the fifth capability we hoped to add, pathwise constraints, closed on a negative.

The four lanes

Main lane owns all claim-bearing decisions; side lanes stop at held-in gates

Main · dynamic retargetingcomplete · 60/60

Build a molecule for 3 edits under goal A, then reveal a previously unknown goal B = potency ∧ developability, and continue from the exact molecule in hand. Five arms branch from an identical switch state. Prefixes were generated in a physically separate phase and hashed before B was ever constructed — that is what proves the history was not steered by knowledge of the future goal.

Two histories lead to the same final requirement, so any difference is attributable to what was pursued first. Potency-first arrives with partial progress on the hard term. Developability-first arrives with the easy term banked and the bottleneck untouched.

Q1 — the intervention works. Twice as large for D-first, which is what it should be: continuing to optimise developability that is already solved does nothing for the conjunction. The clearest single row in the panel is D-first's ignore-the-switch arm — it holds developability 30/30, reaches potency 1/30, and satisfies the conjunction 1/30. It perfectly maintains the requirement it was given and never touches the one that arrived.

Q2 is the stateful claim, and it holds in both histories. Continuing from the realized molecule beats discarding it and restarting with the same remaining budget — unanimously for potency-first. For developability-first it survives at 22/30 with 8 genuine losses, which makes it a real effect rather than a structural one, and it holds even though that prefix solved only the easy half of the eventual goal.

Q3 — there is a real price of surprise. The clairvoyant reference wins both, and by more for D-first. Knowing the goal from step zero is worth something we do not fully recover. That is the honest version, and a better result than "retargeting always wins".

A smoke on 2 sources suggested D-first would beat clairvoyant. It was recorded as a phenomenon to test rather than a hypothesis; at n=30 it reverses cleanly, with clairvoyant winning 24/30.

Rθ moved the least and cycled the most. Perfect envelope retention — but a process that barely moves wins fidelity trivially, which is precisely why mobility had to be a co-equal axis rather than a footnote. The number to interrogate is cycling: 0.281 revisit / 0.300 reversal against ~0.01 for the unlearned family baseline. Rθ returns to states it has already visited, and undoes its own edits, roughly 25× more often.

Two mitigating facts. Uniform's higher mobility comes with +2.31 heavy atoms versus +0.06 — it is mostly just growing molecules, which is cheap movement the suite reports heavy-atom change specifically to expose. And this is not operator collapse: Rθ used all 8 edit families, uniform only 6.

At n=8 this settles nothing. It does suggest the eventual frontier is more likely incomparable than a clean win, and it is the direction a pathological-cycling stop rule would watch.

Lane 2 · pathwise constraintsclosed · premise false

Can a structural motif be required to survive at every committed state, not merely in the endpoint? Exact atom- and bond-labeled subgraph preservation, not fingerprint similarity. The premise: endpoint-only filtering can return a valid final molecule after traversing states the requirement forbids.

The premise gate failed, and the mechanism is the interesting part. Of 42 rollouts on the unconstrained support, 19 broke the protected motif and 0 recovered by the end. The two sets are disjoint — every trajectory that broke the motif stayed broken. The frozen kernel can open a labeled ring system but effectively cannot rebuild one within 6 edits.

Motif destruction is absorbing at this horizon, so endpoint validity implies path validity. Endpoint-only filtering is sufficient here by dynamics, not by luck.

The gate that fired is the one that matters: 0 of 19 endpoint-valid rollouts were path-invalid, against a preregistered threshold of 0.10. Every other gate passed — the mask leaves room to act (removing 22.6% of legal successors on average, never emptying the support) and the arms land differently 6/6. A mask that genuinely restricts the support but never changes what endpoint-only returns is precisely the result.

Nothing was tuned in response. The motif rule, horizon, label semantics and panel are unchanged, and the five tuning moves considered were rejected by name in the decision log.

Secondary, deliberately not promoted: endpoint-only returned nothing on 2 of 6 sources because every rollout ended motif-invalid, while masked arms succeeded 6/6. That is a different and weaker claim — absorption plus wasted budget, not excursion-and-return — on 2 events.

Lane 3 · external baselinescuts against

Verified capability registry built from official papers and code, with every claim cited or marked unverified. The finding that matters:

REINVENT 4 changes the objective mid-run, natively. Staged/curriculum learning is a first-class run mode, with per-stage scoring functions and a checkpoint carried into the next stage.

We therefore cannot write "no existing method changes objective mid-run." What survives is narrower and, I think, still the interesting claim: REINVENT re-targets a policy. There is no in-flight molecule to continue from, switching happens at stage boundaries rather than an arbitrary chosen step, and the agent re-adapts by further RL — which is retraining. The defensible claim is continuation from a realized in-flight molecule under a newly revealed objective, with no retraining.

Every other method verified negative on mid-run switching with specific code evidence. Separately, GraphXForm's masking is genuinely pathwise but only over valence and bonding legality — its ring and bonding-pattern constraints are a terminal filter, and there is no substructure matching in the repository at all. The strongest published graph-editing comparator therefore instantiates Lane 2's endpoint-only arm, not its pathwise arm.

One correction against us: DDSBM does support effective atom birth/death via a padded dummy atom type; our related-work matrix marks it as unsupported and should be amended.

Open decisions

Where outside judgement would help most

1 · The oracle-counting convention

Undecided and not neutral. Our rule counts every oracle evaluation including rejects; the PMO benchmark counts unique valid canonical SMILES only. Under the first, MCMC-style baselines look expensive and policy-gradient ones cheap; under the second, the reverse — and published PMO numbers are only comparable under the second. This is a claim-level choice that blocks every external comparison.

2 · How hard to push on Rθ's cycling

If the cycling result survives to a larger panel, does it weaken the middle layer of the thesis enough to warrant reopening the base-process question — or is a process that stays inside the chemical envelope while exploring locally exactly what a well-behaved reference law should look like? Note this sits oddly beside Lane 2's finding that motif destruction is irreversible: the same process apparently cycles among states it can reach, yet cannot return across a ring-system boundary once crossed.

3 · Whether the narrowed novelty claim is enough

Given REINVENT 4, the retargeting claim narrows to in-flight molecular continuation without retraining — now supported by a clean development result. But the pathwise-constraint lane, which was to be the second pillar, has closed on a negative. Does exact target recovery plus dynamic retargeting carry the "one frozen process, many controls" thesis on its own?

Method notes

Practices adopted after three failures in one day

Three statistics were reported and withdrawn today, all the same shape — a quantity whose sign was fixed before any data existed. An action selected by argmax VG and then scored by VG. A "verified" arm that was secretly greedy, making headroom zero by construction. A sign test against a null that policy improvement makes false.

Before reporting any statistic, ask what value it could take if the hypothesis were false. If the answer is "none", it is not a measurement.

This is now enforced in each lane by executable gates rather than prose — including a test that a gate can return FAIL, on the principle that a gate which can only say PASS is not a gate.

Operationally: three network outages cost zero completed work, because every task commits a durable shard and every relaunch skips what exists. Notably, Modal's --detach flag did not survive client-side DNS failure — twice. Durable shards plus resume is the mechanism that actually holds.

Development-stage results. Nothing here except the sealed panel is confirmatory. Held-out data unopened in all three side lanes.
