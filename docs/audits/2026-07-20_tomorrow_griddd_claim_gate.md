# Tomorrow's GrIDDD comparison: claim and evidence gate

**Date:** 2026-07-20  
**Purpose:** prevent a pilot result from being presented as a broader conditional-generation win.

## Claim ladder

### Claim A: COMPOSE beats GrIDDD on QED molecular optimization

This is the narrowest defensible win and the primary target for tomorrow.

Required matched protocol:

- 800 fixed starting molecules with QED in `[0.70, 0.80]`;
- 20 native direct-conditioned candidates per start;
- success if at least one candidate has QED in `[0.90, 1.00]` and Morgan
  Tanimoto similarity at least `0.40` to the starting molecule;
- success denominator is all 800 starts;
- GrIDDD published success is **45.1%** and published diversity is **0.283**;
- COMPOSE must exceed 45.1% with uncertainty reported, while maintaining
  competitive diversity and reporting every invalid, stalled, or exhausted
  attempt.

COMPOSE additionally reports endpoint and pathwise validity/connectivity,
all-attempt uniqueness, wall time, and event count. These are distinguishing
measurements, not replacements for the matched success criterion.

The exact 800-row artifact generated by the released GrIDDD preprocessing is
not public. Until it is recovered, the presentation must label results on the
exact public Jin 800 as **public-Jin-800 protocol** or the frozen GrIDDD-code
reconstruction as **reconstructed GrIDDD preprocessing**. A preliminary result
may be compared with the published 45.1% reference line, but it may not be
called an exact same-lead reproduction.

### Claim B: COMPOSE is a competitive conditional molecular generator

QED lead optimization alone does not establish this broader claim because it
is editing under a similarity constraint rather than de-novo property
targeting. Add at least one native de-novo property-targeting result.

The most efficient companion is ZINC QED targeting:

- report target-versus-realized QED, MAE, validity/connectivity, uniqueness,
  and diversity across the declared target grid;
- GrIDDD reports QED MAE **0.04** and validity **87.2%**;
- show a monotone target-response curve, not only cherry-picked endpoints.

For the full paper, MW targeting is especially valuable because GrIDDD uses it
as its flexible-size test. Its reported MW MAE is **4.89** with **84.2%**
validity. LogP, QM9 dipole moment, and HOMO can follow after the QED proof.

### Claim C: COMPOSE broadly outperforms GrIDDD

This requires multiple matched tasks, not one QED number:

- de-novo property targeting, preferably QED and MW at minimum;
- QED similarity-constrained optimization;
- ideally LogP and DRD2 optimization;
- matched validity, diversity, candidate budget, compute, and failure
  denominators.

Do not use Claim C for tomorrow unless this task panel exists.

## Tomorrow's presentation package

The most credible achievable package is:

1. a compact unconditional-sufficiency slide;
2. a QED constrained-optimization table with the 45.1% GrIDDD reference line;
3. a small native de-novo QED target-response curve;
4. a separate valid-state controller/combined-mechanism panel with exact QED
   call accounting;
5. representative valid trajectories showing anytime candidates and retained
   similarity.

Direct conditioning is the primary GrIDDD comparison. Controller and combined
arms are reported separately because their intermediate QED evaluations use
additional oracle calls that GrIDDD's native direct sampler does not.

If only a subset of the 800 starts finishes, label it a **preliminary fixed-lead
pilot**, show the number of starts and a confidence interval, and do not claim
that the full benchmark has been beaten.

## Conditional benchmark portfolio

The full COMPOSE conditional story should cover different capabilities rather
than repeating variants of QED:

| Capability | Efficient benchmark | GrIDDD reference | Why it matters |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Similarity-constrained editing | QED optimization, 800 leads x 20 candidates | 45.1% success; 0.283 diversity | Direct headline comparison and retained-structure editing |
| De-novo drug-likeness targeting | ZINC QED target response | 0.04 MAE; 87.2% validity | Establishes conditional generation rather than lead editing alone |
| Flexible-size conditional control | ZINC molecular-weight targeting | 4.89 MAE; 84.2% validity | Directly tests learned growth and shrinkage |
| Composition/bonding control | ZINC Crippen LogP targeting | 0.19 MAE; 87.9% validity | Requires chemically meaningful atom/bond changes, not only size control |
| Constrained property improvement | Penalized LogP at similarities 0.4 and 0.6 | 2.70 and 1.33 mean improvement | Standard optimization benchmark, but vulnerable to size exploitation and therefore secondary to QED/MW |
| Learned biological-oracle control | DRD2 at similarity 0.4 | 5.0% success; 0.121 diversity | Demonstrates general oracle steering but introduces an additional learned-oracle provenance burden |
| Quantum-property targeting | QM9 dipole moment and HOMO | 0.66/0.37 MAE; 79.0/94.0% validity | Broader evidence, but requires the QM9 task/data stack and should not delay the exact-RDKit suite |

For tomorrow, the shortest high-information sequence is **QED optimization ->
de-novo QED -> molecular weight -> LogP**. The latter three reuse deterministic
RDKit labels and the same conditional adapter. DRD2 and QM9 quantum targets are
full-paper extensions, not tonight's critical path.

Beyond GrIDDD, scaffold preservation/decoration, multi-objective control, and
matched oracle-efficiency curves are especially aligned with COMPOSE's valid
editor. They strengthen the eventual paper but do not replace the direct
GrIDDD tasks above.

## Ring diagnostic attached to the conditional result

Stratify the fixed leads as acyclic, isolated-ring, and fused-or-bridged. For
each stratum report:

- legal ring grow/delete/restate availability;
- realized ring rewrite use;
- success and QED improvement;
- similarity, validity/connectivity, and candidate diversity.

This determines whether unconditional ring undercoverage limits QED control.
Global corpus fused-ring frequency is not itself a conditional success metric.
