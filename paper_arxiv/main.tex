\composetitle{COMPOSE: Generator Matching over Stochastic Rewrites for
Trans-Dimensional Molecular Generation and Control}
\composeauthors{Anonymous Authors}
\composeabstract{%
Generative models for molecular graphs commonly learn to reverse a convenient
corruption on an ambient representation. Such transitions need not be
executable chemical edits, and intermediate states need not be complete
molecules. We introduce \emph{Rewrite Generator Matching} (RGM), a framework
that instead learns a time-inhomogeneous marked jump process over the
state-dependent fiber of legal executable rewrites. A total hazard sets the
instantaneous event rate, and a conditional mark law selects which rewrite
fires. We instantiate RGM as COMPOSE, a compositional molecular process over
stochastic executable rewrites. COMPOSE operates on disjoint cardinality
strata, including a distinguished null source that is not a molecule. Its
marks create, delete, restate, reconnect, and cyclize molecular structure,
while the executor keeps every non-null committed state inside a declared
space of connected, valence-valid molecules. Because different marks can
execute to the same molecule, we define the molecular transition law as the pushforward of the
learned mark law through execution and canonical molecular identity. This
successor kernel is invariant to successor-preserving re-encodings that
preserve aggregate successor rates and productive-event disposition, as are
controllers that are constant within each successor fiber. Finite-horizon
conditioning, hard support constraints, and dynamic retargeting act on this
kernel. Exact control is claimed only on bounded enumerable state spaces;
learned values approximate it at molecular scale. Source-conditioned editing
and timed de novo generation require separate checkpoints and inference
regimes. Graph validity does not imply synthetic feasibility.}
\end{composefrontmatter}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Much of modern generative modeling begins by choosing a tractable corruption
process and learning to reverse it. Gaussian noise, categorical replacement,
and continuous-time discrete corruption have produced powerful general
formalisms \citep{ho2020ddpm,austin2021d3pm,campbell2022ctdd}; their graph
instantiations place probability on represented node and edge states
\citep{vignac2023digress,qin2025defog}. For molecular design, however, the
transition mechanism is more than mathematical scaffolding. A useful
intermediate should be a molecule on which a property model, constraint, or
chemist can act, and a transition should specify an operation that can actually
be executed. Generic corruptions do not promise either property: intermediate
graphs need not belong to the molecular state space, and a tensor change need
not correspond to a chemically meaningful edit.

We ask whether the generative process itself can instead be a stochastic
program of executable rewrites. The premise is simple. When a domain supplies
typed rewrite rules, state-dependent application conditions, and a
deterministic executor, those objects can define the infinitesimal events of a
learned jump process. The model then learns transport through operations that
already possess domain meaning, rather than learning to undo a process chosen
only for analytical convenience. This changes the role of an edit vocabulary:
it is not merely a proposal mechanism attached to an optimizer, but the legal
support of the generative process.

\paragraph{Generator Matching over executable marks.}
We formalize this idea as \emph{Rewrite Generator Matching} (\rgm). At state
$x$, the rewrite system exposes a finite, state-dependent action fiber
$\A(x)$. A mark $a\in\A(x)$ is a complete executable operation: a rule,
concrete match or address, and typed payload. The deterministic executor
$\T(x,a)$ returns its successor. The model factorizes the marked rate as
\begin{equation}
  \lambda_\theta(a\mid x,t)
  =
  \Lambda_\theta(x,t)\,p_\theta(a\mid x,t),
  \qquad
  a\in\A(x),
\end{equation}
where $\Lambda_\theta$ is the total event hazard and $p_\theta$ is the
conditional mark law. Its generator acts on test functions by
$\sum_{a\in\A(x)}\lambda_\theta(a\mid x,t)
[f(\T(x,a))-f(x)]$. Generator Matching supplies the rate-fitting principle
\citep{holderrieth2025generator}; the executable rewrite system supplies the
state space, event fibers, legal support, and endpoint-conditioned programs
from which teacher events are drawn. The learned object is therefore a marked
stochastic rewrite process, not an unconstrained prediction followed by
repair.

\paragraph{A genuinely trans-dimensional molecular process.}
We call the molecular instantiation \method: a
\textbf{CO}mpositional \textbf{M}olecular \textbf{P}rocess
\textbf{O}ver \textbf{S}tochastic \textbf{E}xecutable rewrites. It instantiates
\rgm on the disjoint union
\[
  \X=\bigsqcup_{n=0}^{N}\X_n,
  \qquad
  \X_0=\{\varnothing\}.
\]
For $n\geq 1$, $\X_n$ contains supported, connected, valence-valid molecular
graphs with $n$ active atoms. The distinguished null state $\varnothing$ is
\emph{not} a molecule; it is a reversible source connected to supported
one-atom states. Production tensors use persistent padded slots, but padding is
only a coordinate system. Atom birth and death change the active graph and
therefore move between different cardinality strata.

The molecular marks create and delete atoms, restate atom type and valence,
change bond order, reroute attached subgraphs, and close or open cycles. The
legal fiber depends on the current
molecule, and every committed action is executed before the successor is
accepted. Consequently, every \emph{non-null} committed state is a complete
molecule and every transition is an executable rewrite. This is the structural
reason a trajectory can support mid-course property evaluation, protected
substructures, interruption, branching, size correction, and topology
redesign. It does not imply synthetic accessibility, and the declared operator
set need not be one-step inverse-closed for every operation.

\paragraph{The molecular law is a pushforward.}
Different executable marks can yield the same molecular successor because of
symmetry, persistent-slot aliases, operand ordering, or alternative internal
refinements. For a canonical molecular successor $y$, define its successor
fiber
\[
  \G_y(x)=\{a\in\A(x):\T(x,a)\cong y\}.
\]
The state-level molecular law is not any one representative mark. Its raw
canonical pushforward is
\[
  \bar p_\theta(y\mid x,t)
  =
  \sum_{a\in\G_y(x)}p_\theta(a\mid x,t),
\]
which may assign mass to canonical self-events or other outcomes excluded by
the registered productive-commit convention. The fixed-budget editing kernel
conditions this law on a productive molecular jump; the formal normalization
appears in Section~\ref{sec:productive-kernel}. This distinction is essential.
Refining or coarsening marks within one successor fiber, while preserving the
aggregate successor rate and productive-event disposition, leaves the
molecular process unchanged. It also leaves any controller that is constant
within canonical-successor fibers unchanged. A mark-level power tilt, top-$k$
rule, nucleus filter, or family-wise bias generally lacks that invariance. We
therefore control and evaluate the process at the molecular-successor level
even when the internal model is factorized over marks.

This transition-level aggregation is closely related to pushforward Generator
Matching for deterministic images of latent processes
\citep{billera2026latent}. That theory is the closest generic precedent; here
aliases are executable event marks, the map
$a\mapsto\operatorname{canon}(\T(x,a))$ depends on the current molecule, and
the aggregated kernel is retained as the molecular control interface.

\paragraph{Control follows from the kernel.}
Because every productive edge in $P_\theta$ is legal and every non-null state
admits molecular evaluation, the induced kernel is an intervention substrate.
Hard path constraints restrict its support; soft objectives reweight supported
successors; and a remaining-budget value permits continuation after an
objective changes. For a fixed kernel $P$, terminal desirability $g$, and
remaining budget $b$, the finite-horizon value
$h_b(x)=\E_P[g(X_b)\mid X_0=x]$ gives the Doob-controlled law
$P_b^g(y\mid x)=P(y\mid x)h_{b-1}(y)/h_b(x)$ whenever $h_b(x)>0$.
The transform never creates an illegal edge, although hard conditioning may
prune base support. We claim exactness only on bounded state spaces that can be
enumerated and audited. At molecular scale, a learned value function is an
approximation and must be calibrated as such.

\paragraph{Two checkpoints, two inference regimes.}
The framework supports two related but distinct uses. Source-conditioned
editing starts from a supplied molecule and follows a fixed-budget embedded
molecular jump chain; it does not use the magnitude of the continuous-time
hazard. Unconditional de novo generation starts from its own source
distribution, includes the reversible null source, and uses a timed
Gillespie-style CTMC \citep{gillespie1977}. These regimes require separate data
mixtures, hazard contracts, checkpoints, and evaluations. An editing
checkpoint is not evidence for unconditional generation, even though both
instantiate the same rewrite framework.

\paragraph{Training and claim boundary.}
The trained random variable and the induced molecular random variable must not
be conflated. The completed 16,000-step editing run optimized the implemented
mark-level Generator-Matching objective, with successor aggregation only where
that implementation explicitly provided it, and remains a diagnostic rather
than a selected paper model. A repaired protocol that optimizes
canonical-successor likelihood is a distinct training protocol; it does not
retroactively make that run successor-trained. In either case, the scientific
definition of the molecular kernel is execution followed by canonical
pushforward, not the particular segmented algorithm used to compute it.
The conditional-loss result of \citet{billera2026latent} applies when the model
and loss parameterize the image-space generator under its assumptions; it does
not turn a selected-mark objective into a successor objective.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
One causal chain organizes the paper: executable state-dependent rewrites lead
to Generator Matching, which yields a trans-dimensional molecular process;
canonical pushforward turns that marked process into a representation-independent
molecular kernel; and that kernel supports exact finite-state and approximate
large-scale control. Our contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Generator Matching over stochastic rewrites.} We formulate a
  learned marked jump process over state-dependent executable action fibers,
  separating total event hazard from the conditional identity and location of
  a rewrite.

  \item \textbf{COMPOSE and the canonical molecular-successor law.} We
  instantiate the framework with molecular birth, death, relabeling,
  reconnection, and compositional cycle operations across cardinality strata,
  and operationalize its molecular control kernel by aggregating executable
  marks through canonical molecular identity.

  \item \textbf{Generation and control on one legal molecular substrate.} We
  provide a common successor-kernel interface for exact finite-horizon
  conditioning on bounded enumerable spaces and for learned approximations to
  dynamic retargeting, Pareto branching, and pathwise constraints at molecular
  scale, while keeping fixed-budget editing and timed de novo generation
  explicitly separate.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Relation to prior work.}
None of atom insertion or deletion, valid molecular editing, graph grammars,
Generator Matching, variable-dimension generation, Pareto optimization, or
Doob control is individually new. Generator Matching supplies the
conditional-to-marginal rate regression principle
\citep{holderrieth2025generator}; pushforward Generator Matching supplies the
closest generic image-process result \citep{billera2026latent}. Neither chooses
an executable molecular state space or rewrite language, and we do not claim
either general theorem as a contribution.

Discrete graph diffusion and flow models provide general node--edge
generation mechanisms
\citep{austin2021d3pm,campbell2022ctdd,vignac2023digress,qin2025defog,
jo2022gdss,jo2023grum}. Jump diffusion is the closest formal precedent for
trajectories that change dimension \citep{campbell2023jump}; GrIDDD, Edit
Flows, and Morph make graph or molecular cardinality changes first-class
\citep{ninniri2025griddd,havasi2025editflows,franke2026morph}. Their presence
is why our claim is not variable cardinality alone. COMPOSE combines guarded
birth and death with relabeling, reconnection, and topology-changing events
whose committed non-null endpoints remain in the declared molecular space.

Molecular grammars and constrained graph processes define structured legal
support \citep{kajino2019mhg,guo2022grammar,madeira2024construct,
ruizbotella2026cocograph}; stochastic graph rewriting supplies rule
applications and continuous-time semantics
\citep{danos2015thermodynamic,behr2021rewriting}. Valid molecular editors and
optimizers already traverse molecular neighborhoods
\citep{xie2021mars,fu2021mimosa,jensen2019graphga,gao2022pmo}, and
reaction-based GFlowNets traverse executable transformations
\citep{koziarski2024rgfn,zhu2026spacegfn}. The contribution here is their
particular formal composition: a Generator-Matched executable rewrite process
with trans-dimensional molecular semantics, a canonical successor
pushforward, and successor-level exact and dynamic control.

\section{Rewrite Generator Matching}
\label{sec:rgm}

This section separates the marked event process that is parameterized by the
network from the molecular state process induced by execution and
canonicalization. The distinction matters whenever several marks share one
successor or an executable mark is a canonical self-event.

\subsection{Executable marked rewrite processes}
\label{sec:marked-process}

\paragraph{Rewrite system.}
Let $\X$ be a countable structured state space. An executable rewrite system is
a triple
\[
  (\X,\A,\T),
\]
where $\A(x)$ is the finite fiber of legal marks at $x$ and
$\T:\{(x,a):a\in\A(x)\}\rightarrow\X$ is a deterministic executor. A mark
contains the complete information required to execute an event: its rule,
concrete match or address, and typed payload. Closure is imposed at this
interface: if $a\in\A(x)$, then $\T(x,a)\in\X$. Application conditions may
therefore depend on the complete current state and the complete proposed
action.

\paragraph{Marked rates.}
The model assigns a total marked-event hazard and a normalized conditional
mark law,
\begin{align}
  \lambda_\theta(a\mid x,t)
  &=
  \Lambda_\theta(x,t)\,p_\theta(a\mid x,t),
  \label{eq:marked-rate}\\
  \sum_{a\in\A(x)}p_\theta(a\mid x,t)
  &=1.
\end{align}
The resulting time-inhomogeneous generator is
\begin{equation}
  (\mathcal L_{\theta,t}f)(x)
  =
  \sum_{a\in\A(x)}
  \lambda_\theta(a\mid x,t)
  \left[f(\T(x,a))-f(x)\right].
  \label{eq:marked-generator}
\end{equation}
Here $\Lambda_\theta$ is the hazard of the \emph{marked event process}. It is
not necessarily the exit hazard of the induced molecular state process:
different marked events may execute to the same canonical state, including
the current state.

\subsection{Mark-level Generator-Matching supervision}
\label{sec:mark-gm}

Training examples are compiled as executable programs connecting endpoints.
Conditioned on a program and a sampled progress state, its scheduling law
provides a teacher mark $a^\star$ and a nonnegative teacher rate $r$. With an
importance weight $w$, the implemented selected-mark Poisson--Bregman loss is,
up to terms independent of $\theta$,
\begin{equation}
  \ell_{\mathrm{mark}}
  =
  w\left[
    \Lambda_\theta(x,t)
    -
    r\left\{
      \log\Lambda_\theta(x,t)
      +
      \log p_\theta(a^\star\mid x,t)
    \right\}
  \right].
  \label{eq:mark-gm-loss}
\end{equation}
For a no-jump row, $r=0$ and only the positive hazard term remains. In
expectation, this Poisson Bregman regression targets conditional marked rates
under the teacher process \citep{holderrieth2025generator}. The compiler must
therefore certify both that $a^\star\in\A(x)$ and that executing it yields the
stored successor.

Equation~\eqref{eq:mark-gm-loss} trains a process over executable marks. It is
sensitive to how teacher mass is divided among equivalent mark encodings. The
completed 16,000-step diagnostic editing run used this selected-mark objective
except where its implementation explicitly aggregated internal aliases; it
was not generally trained by canonical-successor likelihood. We retain that
fact as a claim boundary rather than reinterpret the historical run after
evaluation.

\subsection{Canonical pushforward and productive molecular jumps}
\label{sec:productive-kernel}

Let $\mathcal Y_{\mathrm{mol}}$ be the set of canonical identities of supported
non-null molecules, and let
$\mathcal Y=\{\varnothing\}\sqcup\mathcal Y_{\mathrm{mol}}$ include the
distinguished null identity. Define $c:\X\rightarrow\mathcal Y$ by
$c(\varnothing)=\varnothing$ and by canonical molecular identity on non-null
states, and define the state-dependent event map
\[
  \pi_x(a)=c(\T(x,a)).
\]
For $y\in\mathcal Y$, the raw pushforward of the mark law is
\begin{equation}
  \bar p_\theta(y\mid x,t)
  =
  \sum_{a\in\A(x):\,\pi_x(a)=y}
  p_\theta(a\mid x,t).
  \label{eq:raw-pushforward}
\end{equation}
This is a normalized distribution over canonical outcomes of marked events,
but it need not be a distribution over \emph{committed molecular jumps}.
Let $\chi_x^+(y)\in\{0,1\}$ be the registered state-only productive-commit
predicate on a canonical outcome. It is zero for $y=c(x)$ and for any
implementation-virtual outcome excluded by the declared editing convention.
The current production state-only kernel treats every non-self canonical
outcome as productive; writing the predicate explicitly prevents that
implementation fact from becoming an unstated mathematical assumption.
Define the productive successor set, its mass, and the complementary virtual
mass by
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal N^+(x)
  =
  \{y:\exists a\in\A(x),\ \pi_x(a)=y,\ \chi_x^+(y)=1\},
  \qquad
  z_\theta(x,t)
  =
  \sum_{y\in\mathcal N^+(x)}
  \bar p_\theta(y\mid x,t),
  \qquad
  v_\theta(x,t)=1-z_\theta(x,t).
  \label{eq:virtual-productive-mass}
\end{equation}
The notation allows $\mathcal N^+(x)$ to depend on the declared support and
commit semantics, although not on an unrecorded previous state. A
history-dependent rejection rule requires an augmented state or a separate
wrapper.

The off-diagonal rate of the committed molecular process is
\begin{equation}
  Q_{\theta,t}^{+}(x,y)
  =
  \chi_x^+(y)\,
  \Lambda_\theta(x,t)\bar p_\theta(y\mid x,t),
  \qquad y\neq c(x),
  \label{eq:molecular-offdiagonal-rate}
\end{equation}
and its exit hazard is
\begin{equation}
  \Lambda_\theta^{\mathrm{prod}}(x,t)
  =
  \Lambda_\theta(x,t)z_\theta(x,t).
  \label{eq:productive-hazard}
\end{equation}
Thus canonical self-events and any other registered virtual outcomes vanish
from the committed state generator even though they remain outcomes in the
raw marked representation.

Source-conditioned editing uses the productive embedded molecular jump
kernel
\begin{equation}
  P_{\theta,t}^{\mathrm{edit}}(y\mid x)
  =
  \frac{\chi_x^+(y)\bar p_\theta(y\mid x,t)}
       {z_\theta(x,t)},
  \qquad y\in\mathcal Y,
  \label{eq:productive-editing-kernel}
\end{equation}
for $y\in\mathcal N^+(x)$ whenever $z_\theta(x,t)>0$, and it is zero
otherwise. If $z_\theta(x,t)=0$, the state is terminal under this convention.
History-dependent rejection, such as suppressing an immediate return to the
preceding molecule, is not part of this state-only base Markov kernel and must
be represented by a separate wrapper or an augmented state.

The scientific definition in
\eqref{eq:raw-pushforward}--\eqref{eq:productive-editing-kernel} is execution
followed by canonical molecular identity, the registered productive
disposition, and, for editing, conditioning on a productive jump. The
production segmented implementation is an efficient evaluation of that
definition. A separate dictionary implementation that enumerates marks,
executes them, canonicalizes successors, classifies their disposition, and
sums by key is used only as a verification oracle.

\paragraph{Within-fiber invariance.}
Suppose an internal mark is refined into several marks that all lie in the
same successor fiber, share the same productive disposition, and preserve
their aggregate successor rate. Then $\bar p_\theta$, $v_\theta$,
$Q_{\theta,t}^{+}$, and $P_{\theta,t}^{\mathrm{edit}}$ are unchanged.
Consequently, any controller that is constant within canonical-successor
fibers is unchanged. This statement does not cover a controller that tilts,
truncates, or regularizes individual marks, nor does it make mark-level
training refinement invariant: either can change when one mark is replaced by
several aliases. Multi-step representation independence additionally requires
consistent canonical-state handling after each committed transition; we state
that quotient-consistency condition in Appendix~\ref{sec:app-compose-assumptions}
rather than assuming it from the one-step identity.

\subsection{Endpoint programs and the population generator target}
\label{sec:population-rgm}

An endpoint-conditioned compiler supplies executable supervision rather than a
decoder used at inference. Let $Z$ denote a sampled source, target, executable
rewrite program, and progress schedule. Conditioned on $Z$ and its progress
state, let $r_t^Z(a\mid x)$ be the nonnegative teacher rate of mark $a$.
Aggregating the teacher by the same executor, canonical identity, and
productive disposition gives
\begin{equation}
  R_t^Z(y\mid x)
  =
  \chi_x^+(y)
  \sum_{a\in\A(x):\,\pi_x(a)=y}
  r_t^Z(a\mid x).
  \label{eq:conditional-successor-rate}
\end{equation}
The Generator-Matching target is the conditional expectation
\begin{equation}
  Q_t^\star(x,y)
  =
  \E\!\left[
    R_t^Z(y\mid X_t)
    \,\middle|\,
    X_t=x
  \right].
  \label{eq:marginal-successor-rate}
\end{equation}
For the Poisson generator
$\phi(u)=u\log u-u$, the population objective is, up to terms independent of
the model,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal L_{\mathrm{pop}}(Q)
  =
  \E\!\left[
    \sum_{y\in\mathcal N^+(X_t)}
    \left\{
      Q_t(X_t,y)
      -
      R_t^Z(y\mid X_t)\log Q_t(X_t,y)
    \right\}
  \right].
  \label{eq:population-successor-gm}
\end{equation}
On every occupied state--successor pair, its unrestricted nonnegative-rate
minimizer is $Q_t^\star$. If a model exactly realizes this marginal generator,
starts from the program mixture's source marginal, and the finite-horizon
forward equation is well posed, it has the same time marginals and endpoint
law as the program mixture. Appendix
~\ref{sec:app-compose-population-proof} gives the short argument. This is the
Generator-Matching fitting principle, not a claim that the compiler path is
optimal or that a finite network reaches the population minimizer. It also
does not describe the completed selected-mark run, whose objective is stated
next.

\subsection{Successor-aware objectives for editing}
\label{sec:successor-objectives}

The implementation exposes three successor-aware losses, and their meanings
are different. For a productive teacher transition $x\rightarrow y^\star$,
the editing-specific identity loss is the importance-weighted negative
log-likelihood under the embedded kernel,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal L_{\mathrm{id}}
  =
  -
  \frac{
    \sum_{i:r_i>0}w_i
    \log
    P_{\theta,t_i}^{\mathrm{edit}}(y_i^\star\mid x_i)
  }{
    \sum_{i:r_i>0}w_i
  }.
  \label{eq:productive-identity-loss}
\end{equation}
It excludes terminal rows and sends no gradient through
$\Lambda_\theta$. It is therefore an embedded-chain likelihood, not a
Generator-Matching rate objective.

The canonical-successor Poisson--Bregman alternative is
\begin{equation}
  \ell_{\mathrm{succGM}}
  =
  w\left[
    \Lambda_\theta^{\mathrm{prod}}(x,t)
    -
    r\log\left\{
      \Lambda_\theta(x,t)
      \bar p_\theta(y^\star\mid x,t)
    \right\}
  \right],
  \label{eq:successor-gm-loss}
\end{equation}
again up to teacher-only constants. This objective matches the off-diagonal
canonical molecular generator and is the successor-aware mode that is
properly described as Poisson--Bregman Generator Matching. A third hybrid adds
a separately weighted total-hazard loss to
\eqref{eq:productive-identity-loss}; it is not generally algebraically equal
to \eqref{eq:successor-gm-loss}.

These choices cannot be hidden behind a common name. Every reported checkpoint
must declare which objective it used. The current successor-aware training
bridge is restricted to bounded development gates; it does not authorize a
full-corpus run. The final editing protocol is selected only after
successor-level micro-overfit and short-pilot evidence establishes that the
chosen loss preserves all load-bearing operator capabilities.

\subsection{Two inference regimes}
\label{sec:inference-regimes}

\paragraph{Fixed-budget editing.}
Editing starts from a supplied molecule and takes a registered number of
productive molecular jumps using
\eqref{eq:productive-editing-kernel}. The magnitude of
$\Lambda_\theta$ is discarded. If model time changes with progress, the
resulting sequence is a budget-indexed family of kernels rather than one
homogeneous transition matrix. This is the base process used for lead
optimization, Pareto branching, dynamic retargeting, and pathwise
constraints.

\paragraph{Timed de novo generation.}
De novo generation is a separate lane with its own source distribution,
training mixture, checkpoint, and hazard validation. It simulates the marked
process in continuous time, or equivalently the molecular process with
off-diagonal rates in \eqref{eq:molecular-offdiagonal-rate} and exit hazard in
\eqref{eq:productive-hazard}, with registered virtual outcomes thinned
consistently. The
declared sampler is exact Gillespie simulation for a piecewise-constant
operational-time schedule. If the network is instead interpreted as varying
continuously inside a time cell, left-boundary rate freezing is an
approximation and requires grid-refinement and endpoint-sensitivity checks.
The framework defines this timed regime, but the current editing checkpoint is
not evidence that its timed semantics or unconditional generation quality
have been validated.

\section{Finite-horizon control on molecular successors}
\label{sec:finite-control}

The productive kernel provides a common control interface because its states
and edges already have molecular meaning. This section states the exact
finite-horizon construction for the editing regime. The result is classical;
the contribution of \method is the executable molecular process to which it
can be applied.

\subsection{Support-preserving successor control}
\label{sec:support-control}

Let $C$ be a predicate on canonical states and let
$\Psi_t(x,y;\omega)\geq0$ be locally bounded over base-supported transitions
on the declared finite horizon. A continuous-time controller acts on the
committed molecular rates by
\begin{equation}
  \widetilde Q_t(x,y)
  =
  \mathbf 1\{C(y)\}\,
  Q_{\theta,t}^{+}(x,y)\,
  \Psi_t(x,y;\omega),
  \qquad y\neq c(x),
  \label{eq:successor-control-law}
\end{equation}
with the diagonal set to the negative outgoing rate. Thus
$\operatorname{supp}\widetilde Q_t(x,\cdot)$ is contained in the base
successor support. If $C\equiv1$ and $\Psi_t$ is positive on every
base-supported edge, the supports are equal. Starting from a state satisfying
a state-local prefix-closed predicate $C$, masking violating successors makes
$C$ hold at every committed state. Appendix
~\ref{sec:app-compose-support-control-proof} gives the direct proof. This
closure claim is about the declared molecular graph space, not
synthesizability or oracle accuracy. A history-dependent constraint still
requires a killed or augmented state.

\subsection{Budget-indexed Doob transform}

Allow the base editing law to depend on the remaining budget. Let
$P_b(y\mid x)$ be the row-stochastic canonical-successor kernel used when
$b\geq 1$ productive edits remain, and let $g:\mathcal Y\to[0,\infty)$ be a
terminal desirability. Define the backward values
\begin{align}
  h_0(x) &= g(x), \\
  h_b(x) &= \sum_y P_b(y\mid x)h_{b-1}(y).
  \label{eq:backward-value}
\end{align}
At a state for which $h_b(x)>0$, the controlled transition is
\begin{equation}
  P_b^g(y\mid x)
  =
  P_b(y\mid x)\frac{h_{b-1}(y)}{h_b(x)}.
  \label{eq:finite-doob}
\end{equation}
The denominator in \eqref{eq:finite-doob} is the value with the
\emph{remaining} budget, not the terminal value $h_0$. If $h_b(x)=0$, the
desired terminal event has zero base probability from $(b,x)$ and the
controlled row is undefined. We report such a state as unreachable rather
than adding numerical mass to unsupported successors.

\paragraph{Exact terminal tilt.}
Equation~\eqref{eq:backward-value} makes
\eqref{eq:finite-doob} row-stochastic. Along a length-$K$ path
$x_{0:K}$, its likelihood ratio relative to the base path telescopes:
\begin{equation}
  \prod_{j=0}^{K-1}
  \frac{
    P_{K-j}^g(x_{j+1}\mid x_j)
  }{
    P_{K-j}(x_{j+1}\mid x_j)
  }
  =
  \frac{g(x_K)}{h_K(x_0)}.
  \label{eq:path-telescope}
\end{equation}
Consequently the terminal law is exactly
\begin{equation}
  \Pr^g(X_K=y\mid X_0=x_0)
  =
  \frac{
    \Pr(X_K=y\mid X_0=x_0)g(y)
  }{
    h_K(x_0)
  }.
  \label{eq:terminal-tilt}
\end{equation}
An indicator desirability therefore gives exact conditioning on a reachable
terminal event. This statement is horizon-specific: a marginal observed
before the declared final budget is generally not the corresponding terminal
tilt.

\paragraph{Support and within-fiber invariance.}
For every reachable $(b,x)$,
\[
  \operatorname{supp}P_b^g(x,\cdot)
  \subseteq
  \operatorname{supp}P_b(x,\cdot).
\]
More precisely, a base edge to $y$ is retained exactly when
$h_{b-1}(y)>0$; the two row supports are equal only when every base successor
has positive next-state value. Thus the transform never creates an illegal
molecular rewrite, but hard conditioning can prune legal base edges. Because
the recursion uses only canonical-successor probabilities, any
probability-preserving refinement within a successor fiber leaves every
$h_b$ and every controlled row unchanged. The same claim would not hold for a
controller applied separately to the refined marks.

\subsection{Dynamic objectives, Pareto branches, and path constraints}

\paragraph{Dynamic continuation.}
Suppose a trajectory reaches molecule $x$ with $b$ edits remaining and the
objective changes from $g$ to $g'$. Recomputing
\eqref{eq:backward-value} for $g'$ and applying
\eqref{eq:finite-doob} from $(b,x)$ produces the exact $g'$-tilted
continuation of the base process from that state. It does not reproduce the
counterfactual trajectory that would have used $g'$ from the original source.
The remaining budget is therefore a necessary controller input, not merely a
sampling parameter.

\paragraph{Pareto branching.}
A shared molecular prefix can be branched toward multiple terminal regions by
using desirabilities $\{g_z\}$ indexed by a preference, reference direction,
or target box. Each branch reweights the same frozen successor kernel and can
contribute its visited molecules to a common nondominated archive. Linear
scalarization is only one possible definition of $g_z$; target-region and
achievement-scalarizing desirabilities permit branches aimed at non-convex or
under-covered portions of an empirical frontier. Whether this improves
hypervolume per oracle call is an empirical question, not a consequence of
the transform.

\paragraph{Hard and pathwise constraints.}
A state-local, prefix-closed constraint can be enforced by removing violating
successors from the legal base row before computing the backward values.
General history-dependent constraints require either a killed process or an
augmented state that records the relevant path statistic; cumulative rewards
require the corresponding Feynman--Kac construction. A terminal desirability
alone does not establish intermediate path feasibility. In all cases, a
zero backward value is a genuine reachability failure under the declared
support, constraint, and budget.

\subsection{Exact verification and molecular-scale approximation}

Exactness requires complete enumeration of the relevant state graph and exact
backward values. We therefore reserve the term \emph{exact} for a bounded
persistent-slot verification slice built with the same executor and canonical
identity as the production process. There we can compare the controlled
terminal distribution with \eqref{eq:terminal-tilt}, audit every support
relation, and verify the backward recursion without Monte Carlo error.

At molecular scale, the value
\[
  h_\phi(b,x;x_{\mathrm{src}},z,m)
  \approx
  \E_P[g_z(X_b)\mid X_0=x]
\]
is learned or estimated, where $x_{\mathrm{src}}$ is the immutable source and
$m$ describes protected structure or other control context. Such a controller
is approximate even though every proposed transition remains a legal
successor. Calibration, backward-equation residual, successor ranking, and
control quality must therefore be measured separately from the exact
finite-state result.
