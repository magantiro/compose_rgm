\section{COMPOSE}
\label{sec:method}

COMPOSE represents molecular design as control of a learned stochastic process
over complete molecules (Figure~\ref{fig:ppp}). An exact rewrite executor
determines which molecular transitions are available, a goal-independent
reference model learns probability over those transitions, and finite-horizon
control reweights the frozen process toward a downstream design objective.

\subsection{Executable molecular state space}
\label{sec:gem-support}

Let
\[
  \mathcal X=\bigcup_{n=1}^{n_{\max}}\mathcal X_n
\]
denote the molecular state space, where $\mathcal X_n$ contains connected
molecular graphs with $n$ heavy atoms, declared atom and bond attributes, and
valid valences. Every $x\in\mathcal X$ is therefore a complete molecule rather
than a masked, corrupted, or partially specified state.

Transitions are typed graph rewrites. An edit mark $a$ specifies a rewrite
family, molecular operands, and a typed payload, and $T(x,a)$ denotes the
resulting graph. From state $x$, the executor exposes
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal A(x)
  =
  \left\{
    a:\; a\text{ matches } x
    \;\text{and}\;
    T(x,a)\text{ passes all executor guards}
  \right\}.
  \label{eq:gem-editset}
\end{equation}
The guards enforce the declared atom and bond vocabularies, valence
constraints, connectivity, and molecular-size bounds before an edit is
committed. Inadmissible states therefore have zero transition support rather
than receiving a learned penalty. The rewrite language includes atom insertion
and deletion together with atom-, bond-, and ring-level transformations
(\cref{app:statespace}). Insertions and deletions connect $\mathcal X_n$ with
$\mathcal X_{n\pm1}$, allowing trajectories to grow, shrink, and restructure
molecules within the same state space.

Because every admitted rewrite passes these guards, $T(x,a)\in\mathcal X$ for
all $x\in\mathcal X$ and $a\in\mathcal A(x)$; the formal closure statement and
full rewrite semantics are given in \cref{app:statespace}. Multi-edit ring construction and
refinement used in molecular optimization compile into the same primitive
rewrite language and do not enlarge this support (\cref{app:macros}).

The edit sets $\mathcal A(x)$ specify the support of the process. We next learn
how probability is distributed across it.

\subsection{Learning the reference process}
\label{sec:gem-reference}

We learn a goal-independent stochastic law over executable edits. Writing a
mark as $a=(k,o,\eta)$, with rewrite family $k$, operands $o$, and typed payload
$\eta$, the reference model factorizes
\begin{equation}
  p_\theta(a\mid x)
  =
  p_\theta(k\mid x)\,
  p_\theta(o,\eta\mid k,x),
  \qquad a\in\mathcal A(x).
  \label{eq:gem-factorization}
\end{equation}
Normalization is restricted to candidates admitted by the executor, and the
model receives no design objective, remaining budget, or task-specific
constraint. The learned law therefore depends on the current molecular state but
not on the downstream purpose for which the process will later be used.

The stochastic object used for control is a distribution over molecular
successors, not raw edit marks. Different marks can produce the same molecule
because of graph symmetries, equivalent matches, or alternative internal rewrite
descriptions. For a canonical successor $y$, define its executable fiber
\[
  \mathcal G_y(x)
  =
  \{a\in\mathcal A(x): T(x,a)\simeq y\},
\]
where $\simeq$ denotes registered molecular canonicalization. Let $\mathcal
S(x)$ denote the distinct non-self canonical successors of $x$. Conditioning the
pushforward mark law on a committed change of molecular state gives
\begin{equation}
  R_\theta(y\mid x)
  =
  \frac{
    \sum_{a\in\mathcal G_y(x)} p_\theta(a\mid x)
  }{
    \sum_{y'\in\mathcal S(x)}
    \sum_{a\in\mathcal G_{y'}(x)} p_\theta(a\mid x)
  },
  \qquad y\in\mathcal S(x).
  \label{eq:gem-rtheta}
\end{equation}
Aggregating over the fiber prevents a molecular transition from receiving
additional probability merely because symmetry or implementation details provide
several equivalent edit descriptions. Control therefore acts on $R_\theta$ after
canonicalization rather than on individual edit marks.

We train $R_\theta$ from executable teacher transitions by minimizing the
canonical-successor negative log-likelihood over transitions that commit a
molecular jump,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal L_{\mathrm{ref}}(\theta)
  =
  -\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\log R_\theta(y_i^\star\mid x_i),
  \label{eq:gem-refloss}
\end{equation}
with rows sampled from the declared editing measure. The implementation carries a
per-row registered transition weight, which is unity for every row of the frozen
checkpoint reported here.
Every teacher transition is replayed through the same executor used at
inference. Targets and compiled programs are used to construct supervision but
are not supplied to the reference model at inference. Once trained, $R_\theta$ is
fixed across downstream objectives.

\subsection{Finite-horizon control of the frozen process}
\label{sec:gem-control}

Task information enters only after the reference process has been learned. Let
$z$ denote a design objective and $g_z:\mathcal X\rightarrow\mathbb R_{\geq0}$
its terminal desirability. For $b$ remaining transitions, define
\begin{equation}
  h_0(x,z)=g_z(x),
  \qquad
  h_b(x,z)
  =
  \sum_{y\in\mathcal S(x)} R_\theta(y\mid x)\, h_{b-1}(y,z)
  =
  \mathbb E_{R_\theta}\!\left[ g_z(X_b)\mid X_0=x \right].
  \label{eq:gem-backward}
\end{equation}
A property model scores the current molecule, whereas $h_b(x,z)$ measures the
desirability reachable from that molecule within the remaining budget.

For $h_b(x,z)>0$, the finite-horizon Doob transform defines
\begin{equation}
  P^{z,b}(y\mid x)
  =
  R_\theta(y\mid x)\,
  \frac{h_{b-1}(y,z)}{h_b(x,z)} .
  \label{eq:gem-central}
\end{equation}
The controlled kernel reweights only transitions already supported by
$R_\theta$.

\begin{proposition}[Finite-horizon molecular control]
\label{prop:gem-doob}
Starting from $x_0$ with budget $K$, applying
Equation~\eqref{eq:gem-central} at each step yields
\[
  \Pr\nolimits_{P^z}(X_K=y\mid X_0=x_0)
  =
  \Pr\nolimits_{R_\theta}(X_K=y\mid X_0=x_0)\,
  \frac{g_z(y)}{h_K(x_0,z)} .
\]
\end{proposition}

Exact backward values therefore realize the $g_z$-tilted terminal law without
introducing transitions outside the reference support. The proof and
terminal-state assumptions are given in \cref{app:control-proof}.

Exact backward evaluation is infeasible in realistic molecular spaces. We
therefore parameterize a nonnegative, goal- and budget-conditioned
approximation $h_\phi(x,z,b)\approx h_b(x,z)$, learned from finite-budget
continuations of the frozen reference process, and substitute it into
Equation~\eqref{eq:gem-central}.
Approximation error can alter probability among supported successors, but cannot
introduce transitions outside $R_\theta$. Exact terminal reweighting is
therefore a property of the exact $h$-transform rather than the learned
approximation. Training and sampling details are given in \cref{app:models,app:sampling}.

Because control is recomputed from the molecule actually reached, its objective
can change during a trajectory. If the process reaches $x_\tau$ under $z$ and the
objective changes to $z'$, control resumes from $x_\tau$ under $z'$ with
$R_\theta$ unchanged. We evaluate this setting in
Section~\ref{sec:gem-exp-capabilities}.
