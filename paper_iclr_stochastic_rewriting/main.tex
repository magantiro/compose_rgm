\begin{abstract}
Most generative models pick a generic corruption process (noise, an absorbing mask, a categorical flip) and learn to reverse it. The corruption is chosen for analytical convenience, its individual steps carry no executable meaning for the object being generated, and that object exists only at the end of the trajectory. We introduce \emph{Rewrite Generator Matching} (\rgm), which performs Generator Matching over stochastic rewrites and so defines the generative process as a stochastic program of executable rewrites: every state carries a state-dependent set of applicable marks, a learned hazard says how fast an event fires, a learned mark law says which one fires, and Generator Matching fits both from endpoint-conditioned rewrite programs. We instantiate \rgm as \method, a compositional molecular process over stochastic executable rewrites. It operates on $\X=\bigsqcup_{n=0}^{N_{\max}}\X_n$ with a distinguished null source at $n=0$. Its marks create and destroy atoms, relabel and revalence them, reroute subgraphs, and close and open rings, so that molecular size, composition, connectivity, and topology are modelled jointly and every non-null committed state is a complete molecule. Execution and canonicalization aggregate equivalent marks (symmetry aliases, alternative addresses, distinct executable lowerings) into a canonical molecular-successor kernel. Under successor-preserving re-encodings that preserve aggregate successor rate, both the induced state process and fiber-constant successor-level controllers are invariant. Control then acts on that kernel rather than being attached afterwards: hard requirements delete transitions, and the finite-horizon Doob transform reweights the ones that remain, realizing exact terminal tilts and exact continuation after a mid-trajectory objective change wherever the state space is enumerable. Graph validity is not synthetic feasibility, and we claim no exactness at scale.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Modern generative modeling rests on a standard move: pick a generic corruption process (Gaussian noise, an absorbing mask, a uniform categorical flip), apply it to an ambient encoding of the data, and learn to reverse it. The corruption is chosen for analytical convenience, its steps mean nothing about the object being generated, and that object typically exists only at the end of the trajectory. We ask instead: \emph{can the generative process itself be a stochastic program of executable rewrites, with learned rates over the actions that are meaningful for the domain?} Where a domain already has a vocabulary of legal edits and an executor, that vocabulary can be the infinitesimal structure of a learned generative process.

We formulate this as \emph{Rewrite Generator Matching} (\rgm): Generator Matching over stochastic rewrites, carried out on an executable rewrite system. Fix typed rewrite rules and an executor. Every state $x$ carries a state-dependent set $\A(x)$ of \emph{applicable marks} (a rule, a concrete match, a typed payload), guarded by application conditions so that the executed successor is again a legal state. \rgm learns a \emph{total hazard} $\lambda_\theta(x,t)$, how fast an event fires, and a \emph{conditional mark law} $p_\theta(a\mid x,t)$, which one fires; their product is a marked jump rate. The trained object is therefore a stochastic process over executable marks. Because several marks can execute to the same object, execution and canonicalization push the marked law forward to a kernel over distinct successor \emph{objects}; that induced state-level process is the molecular control substrate. Generator Matching \citep{holderrieth2025generator} supplies the fitting principle: endpoint-conditioned rewrite programs give conditional generators whose conditional expectation given the current state is the marginal generator, recovered by Poisson--Bregman regression on rates. Inference needs no endpoint, program, candidate bank, beam, or terminal repair.

Molecules are where this pays off most sharply. We call the molecular instantiation \method: a \textbf{CO}mpositional \textbf{M}olecular \textbf{P}rocess \textbf{O}ver \textbf{S}tochastic \textbf{E}xecutable rewrites. Its state space is \emph{trans-dimensional}: for $n\ge1$, $\X_n$ contains connected, valence-valid molecular graphs with $n$ active heavy atoms, while $\X_0=\{\varnothing\}$ contains a distinguished reversible null source that is not a molecule. \method generates on $\X=\bigsqcup_{n=0}^{N_{\max}}\X_n$, and its marks are classified by their effect on the stratum: \emph{birth} ($\X_n\to\X_{n+1}$), \emph{death} ($\X_n\to\X_{n-1}$, with a connected non-null remainder or the registered one-atom-to-null transition), and \emph{same-cardinality} (relabel and revalence an atom, change a bond order, reroute a subgraph, close or open a cycle, restate a ring's electronics). Size, composition, connectivity, and topology are therefore modelled jointly, and a trajectory can overshoot and correct rather than committing to a cardinality before generation begins. Because the rewrites are guarded, \emph{every non-null step is a molecule and every transition is an executable rewrite}: at each non-null committed state, an oracle query, a hard constraint, or a chemist's inspection is meaningful. Everything that follows (pathwise constraints, mid-trajectory intervention, branching, exact retargeting) uses that property, and we do not restate it.

\paragraph{The causal chain.}
One thesis runs through the paper, and each arrow is a section: \emph{executable state-dependent rewrites $\to$ Generator Matching (\cref{sec:rgm}) $\to$ a learned trans-dimensional molecular process (\cref{sec:compose}) $\to$ a canonical molecular-successor kernel (\cref{sec:quotient-section}) $\to$ exact and dynamic stochastic control (\cref{sec:control}).} The order matters: most of what looks like a chemistry capability is a property of the kernel, and the kernel is a property of the rewrite system. Source-conditioned lead optimization is the flagship \emph{application} (experiment~G), not the definition of the framework.

\paragraph{What we do not claim as new.}
Atom insertion and deletion, valid molecular editing, Generator Matching, Doob transforms, variable-dimension generation, and graph grammars are all established; \cref{sec:related} places each. The defensible claim is their formal combination and interface: a generator-matched stochastic graph-rewrite process whose state-dependent marks create, delete, relabel, reconnect, and cyclize molecular structure; whose legal support is closed over molecular states; whose equivalent action encodings are quotiented into a molecular kernel; and whose resulting process supports exact finite-horizon control.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,topsep=2pt,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Generator Matching over stochastic rewrites}: Generator Matching over state-dependent executable rewrite marks, with a learned total hazard and conditional mark law fitted by a Poisson--Bregman objective from certified endpoint-conditioned programs. Stated for any executable rewrite system, not only for chemistry.
    \item \textbf{Trans-dimensional molecular generation and the canonical successor quotient}: a process on $\bigsqcup_{n=0}^{N_{\max}}\X_n$ that creates and destroys typed graph entities and rebuilds ring topology compositionally, with every non-null committed state molecular; equivalent marks are aggregated into a molecular kernel, which we prove is invariant under successor-preserving re-encodings that preserve aggregate successor rate, as are fiber-constant successor-level controllers.
    \item \textbf{Control of the resulting molecular kernel}: the shared framework supports separately trained \emph{de novo} and source-conditioned editing processes; on an induced editing kernel we give exact finite-horizon Doob control with exact continuation after a mid-trajectory objective change and pathwise support constraints, and we specify a goal-conditioned learned controller that will be evaluated and calibrated as an approximation.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work and positioning}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Generator Matching and graph generative processes.}
Generator Matching \citep{holderrieth2025generator} regresses a marginal generator from tractable endpoint-conditional generators; it prescribes no state space, transitions, or coupling, and \rgm supplies those for rewrite systems. Discrete graph diffusion and flow models corrupt node and edge categories and learn a denoiser \citep{vignac2023digress,austin2021d3pm,campbell2022ctdd,qin2025defog}; their intermediates are discrete graphs but need not be connected or valence-admissible, so an oracle query at an intermediate is not chemically defined.

\paragraph{Variable cardinality and trans-dimensional processes.}
Trans-dimensional generative modeling via jump diffusion learns processes moving between spaces of different dimension \citep{campbell2023jump}, the closest formal precedent for our state space, and the reason we claim no novelty for variable-dimension generation; the distinction is that our dimension changes are \emph{executable typed graph edits} with application conditions rather than jumps in an ambient space. GrIDDD adds monotone node insertion and deletion \citep{ninniri2025griddd}, Edit Flows builds insertion/deletion/substitution flows \citep{havasi2025editflows}, Morph changes 3D cardinality by unbalanced optimal transport \citep{franke2026morph}, and DDSBM learns CTMC bridges for graph transformation \citep{kim2025ddsbm}. \method differs in that one jump is a guarded chemical rewrite between complete molecules, in both directions of cardinality, with topology-changing operators alongside.

\paragraph{Validity by construction, editors, and control.}
Constrained graph diffusion projects onto restricted property classes: edge-deletion-invariant topology for ConStruct, aggregate counts for PRODIGY, fixed formula and degree for CoCoGraph \citep{madeira2024construct,sharma2024prodigy,ruizbotella2026cocograph}; molecular grammars generate through legal productions \citep{kajino2019mhg,guo2022grammar}; reaction-based GFlowNets traverse executable transformations \citep{koziarski2024rgfn,zhu2026spacegfn}. Stochastic graph rewriting is the closest operational precedent, defining matches, application conditions, and a CTMC from contextual propensities \citep{danos2015thermodynamic,behr2021rewriting}; we import that rule/rate separation and replace fixed propensities with a learned time-inhomogeneous generator. A separate line edits valid molecules directly \citep{xie2021mars,fu2021mimosa,jensen2019graphga,gao2022pmo}; we claim no novelty there. MARS and MIMOSA couple learned or structured edit proposals to task-conditioned MCMC-style optimization, while \method learns an objective-independent reference transport that is subsequently controlled. Doob $h$-transforms are classical, and inference-time controllers are instances of $\Psi$ in \eqref{eq:control-law}, which we run on our own kernel so a comparison isolates the control rule rather than the base model. \Cref{app:related} expands each of these and \cref{tab:positioning} is the property-by-property ledger.



\section{Generator Matching over stochastic rewrites}
\label{sec:rgm}

\rgm is stated for any executable rewrite system; we develop it on the molecular state space that \cref{sec:compose} instantiates. Generic objects: the state set, applicable marks, hazard, mark law, successor aggregation, and objective. Chemistry-specific: the grammar, rules, executor, and source processes.

\paragraph{States, marks, and the applicable set.}
Given a declared grammar $\Gamma$ and an entity cap $N_{\max}$, let $\M=\{\varnothing\}\sqcup\{G:1\le|V(G)|\le N_{\max},\ G$ connected and admissible under $\Gamma\}$. The distinguished null state $\varnothing$ is a reversible source, not a molecule. A rule $r\in\Rset$ has a left pattern, a right pattern, and application conditions; a \emph{mark} $a=(r,m,\xi)$ is a rule, a match $m\in\Match_r(G)$, and a typed payload, and its executor $\T_a$ is partial. The \emph{applicable set} is
\begin{equation}
\A(G)=\{(r,m,\xi):m\in\Match_r(G),\;\legal(G,r,m,\xi)=1\},
\qquad \T_aG\in\M\;\;\forall a\in\A(G),
\label{eq:legal-fiber}
\end{equation}
so legality is state-dependent and closure holds by construction rather than by post-hoc repair.

\paragraph{The two learned objects.}
\rgm factorizes the marked rate into a scalar hazard and a conditional mark law,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
q_\theta(a\mid G,t)=\lambda_\theta(G,t)\,p_\theta(a\mid G,t),
\qquad \lambda_\theta(G,t)\ge0,\\
\textstyle\sum_{a\in\A(G)}p_\theta(a\mid G,t)=1
\qquad\text{when }\A(G)\neq\emptyset
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:hazard-marklaw}
\end{equation}
We adopt the terminal convention $\lambda_\theta(G,t)=0$, $q_\theta(\cdot\mid G,t)=0$ when $\A(G)=\emptyset$; the conditional law is then unused. The hazard is a rate in events per unit operational time and governs \emph{when}; the mark law governs \emph{which}. Only a conditioned mark law is needed to walk the productive embedded jump chain. The editing and \emph{de novo} regimes therefore share this factorization and executor, but use separately fitted checkpoints and different inference semantics (\cref{sec:sampling}). The marked generator is $(L_{\theta,t}f)(G)=\sum_{a\in\A(G)}q_\theta(a\mid G,t)[f(\T_aG)-f(G)]$. Since several marks may execute to the same object, we write $\Gset_H(G)=\{a\in\A(G):\T_aG\simeq H\}$ for the \emph{successor fiber} of $H$ and aggregate,
\begin{align}
\Sset(G)=\{H:\Gset_H(G)\neq\emptyset\},\qquad
Q_{\theta,t}(G,H)\;=\!\!\sum_{a\in\Gset_H(G)}\!\! q_\theta(a\mid G,t),\\
\Praw(H\mid G,t)\;=\!\!\sum_{a\in\Gset_H(G)}\!\! p_\theta(a\mid G,t).
\label{eq:quotient-rate}
\end{align}
The raw pushforward includes every executable mark, including canonical self or implementation-virtual events. Let $\Sset^+(G)\subseteq\Sset(G)$ be the distinct successors admitted by the registered productive-commit predicate, which excludes those events, and define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
Z_\theta^+(G,t)&=\sum_{J\in\Sset^+(G)}\Praw(J\mid G,t),\\
\Pmol(H\mid G,t)&=
\frac{\one\{H\in\Sset^+(G)\}\Praw(H\mid G,t)}{Z_\theta^+(G,t)}
\quad\text{when }Z_\theta^+(G,t)>0.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:productive-kernel}
\end{equation}
Thus $\Praw$ is the executor-and-canonicalization pushforward of the mark law, while $\Pmol$ is the productive embedded molecular jump kernel used for editing. If $Z_\theta^+=0$, the production sampler halts and records a stall; finite-horizon algebra represents that declared terminal convention with an absorbing row rather than renormalizing nonexistent productive mass. \Cref{sec:quotient-section} develops both objects as first-class constructions.

\paragraph{Latent rewrite programs as conditional paths.}
Sample a source--target pair from a coupling with marginals $\pzero$ and $\pdata$, and let a randomized compiler emit a certified program $Z=(G_0,a_1,\ldots,a_K,G^Z_K)$ with every $G^Z_{k}\in\M$ and $G^Z_K\simeq G_1$. Conditional on $Z$, define a progress variable $K_t$ with a binomial marginal and $X_t=G^Z_{K_t}$; the pure-birth realization fires only the next instruction, giving a conditional marked rate $r^Z_t(a\mid k)$ that is nonzero only for $a=a_{k+1}$ and, in operational time, equals the number of remaining instructions (\cref{app:model}). Sampling $k$ from its marginal avoids simulating the teacher chain. Writing $R^Z_t(H\mid X_t,k)=\sum_{a\in\Gset_H(X_t)}r^Z_t(a\mid k)$, the object to be learned is the marginal generator $Q_t(G,H)=\E[R^Z_t(H\mid X_t,K_t)\mid X_t=G]$, a conditional expectation inherited from Generator Matching; the rewrite state space, program construction, applicable sets, and successor aggregation are what \rgm contributes.

\paragraph{Objective.}
For $\phi(u)=u\log u-u$, the Bregman divergence between nonnegative rates gives, up to target-only constants,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{RGM}}(\theta)=
\E_{t,Z,K_t}\Big[\textstyle\sum_{H\in\Sset(X_t)}
\Big\{Q_{\theta,t}(X_t,H)-R^Z_t(H\mid X_t,K_t)\log Q_{\theta,t}(X_t,H)\Big\}\Big].
\label{eq:gm-objective}
\end{equation}
The first term calibrates total firing mass, the second rewards the teacher successor, and unlike a classification loss over the observed action it is proper for rates.

Three objective identities must remain distinct. Equation~\eqref{eq:gm-objective} is the general successor-rate population objective used for the \rgm consistency result below. The completed 16,000-step diagnostic run instead optimized the implemented mark-level objective
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{hist}}=
\E\!\left[\lambda_\theta(X_t,t)-r_t^Z\!\left\{\log\lambda_\theta(X_t,t)+\log p_\theta(a^\star\mid X_t,t)\right\}\right],
\label{eq:historical-mark-objective}
\end{equation}
with the teacher mark $a^\star$ selected by the compiled program (and graft aliases already grouped). The editing-V2 lane declares productive canonical-successor identity training,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{edit\text{-}V2}}
=
\E\!\left[-w\,\log \Pmol(H^\star\mid X_t,t)\right]
+\alpha_{\mathrm{haz}}\,\mathcal L_{\mathrm{haz}},
\label{eq:editing-v2-objective}
\end{equation}
where $H^\star$ is the teacher molecule, $w$ is the frozen sampling correction, and any hazard loss and coefficient are separately registered and checkpoint-bound. The editing identity term does not silently inherit the historical mark objective, and the timed \emph{de novo} lane retains its own calibrated rate objective.

\begin{assumption}[Finite closed rewrite system, consistent programs]\label{ass:finite}
$N_{\max}$ and all type vocabularies are finite; every committed action satisfies \eqref{eq:legal-fiber}; the total rate is bounded on the finite horizon; and the program sampler has endpoint marginals $G_0\sim\pzero$, $G^Z_K\sim\pdata$ with every sampled program executable and terminating at its target.
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}[Population minimizer; endpoint law]\label{prop:gm}
Under \cref{ass:finite}, for every $(G,t)$ occupied by the program mixture and every $H\in\Sset(G)$, the unrestricted nonnegative-rate minimizer of \eqref{eq:gm-objective} is the marginal aggregated rate $Q_t(G,H)$ defined above. If in addition $Q_{\theta,t}=Q_t$ on occupied states, and the learned process starts at $\pzero$, then its time marginals equal the program-mixture marginals and its terminal law is $\pdata$.
\end{proposition}

In words: fitting rates by \eqref{eq:gm-objective} recovers exactly the state-level generator of the program mixture, and an exactly-fitted model transports $\pzero$ to $\pdata$. Neither statement is chemistry-specific, and neither implies that a finite network attains the population minimizer or that teacher programs are optimal paths. \emph{Sketch:} the objective decouples across $(G,t,H)$ into $u-\E[Y]\log u$, strictly convex with minimizer $\E[Y]$; the endpoint claim follows from uniqueness of the finite-state forward solution (\cref{app:proofs}).

The framework is now fixed; what remains is to choose the rewrite system, and that choice is where the molecular content lives.

\section{COMPOSE: a trans-dimensional molecular rewrite process}
\label{sec:compose}

\subsection{A trans-dimensional state space}
\label{sec:transdim}

Molecules of different sizes are different objects, and a process honest about that must move between spaces of different dimension. For $n\ge1$, let $\X_n$ be the connected, valence-admissible molecular graphs with exactly $n$ \emph{active} heavy atoms; let $\X_0=\{\varnothing\}$ contain the distinguished null state. Then
\begin{equation}
\X=\bigsqcup_{n=0}^{N_{\max}}\X_n,
\label{eq:transdim}
\end{equation}
so that the state space $\M$ of \cref{sec:rgm} is exactly $\X$. The null graph is not a molecule, but production treats it as a reversible source, $\varnothing\rightleftarrows$ one-atom molecules. Every applicable mark is a \emph{birth} ($\X_n\to\X_{n+1}$), a \emph{death} ($\X_n\to\X_{n-1}$), or \emph{same-cardinality} ($\X_n\to\X_n$), and \cref{tab:operators} classifies the operator registry accordingly.

\paragraph{The slot array is a coordinate system, not the state.}
The implementation stores a state in a fixed array of $N_{\max}$ persistent slots, and it matters that the array is a \emph{coordinate system} while the semantic state is the \emph{active molecular graph}. A birth does not flip a padding indicator: it creates a typed graph entity that participates in valence, connectivity, ring perception, and canonicalization, and the successor is a different molecule in a different stratum. Two states with the same active subgraph in different slots are the same state, and the canonical successor map of \cref{sec:quotient-section} performs that identification. Persistent slots are semantic action \emph{addresses}: serialized trajectories therefore store the exact slot-indexed graph state rather than reconstructing it from a canonical string, which preserves molecular identity but not action coordinates. The generative consequence is that cardinality is not a prior draw: fixed-cardinality processes commit to $n$ before generating, insertion-only processes can grow but never revise, whereas this process adapts cardinality to what the trajectory has built, including to an objective supplied only at inference. Experiment~C (\cref{tab:expdesign}) tests exactly that.

The bounded rebuild currently tests eight development families: atom insertion, deletion, and restatement; bond reordering; graft; cycle closing and opening; and coordinated ring-system restatement. Whole-ring deletion and the legacy whole-ring growth macro are disabled in this pilot. This \emph{Active8} basis is not yet the final production support: the operator set is frozen only after registered reachability, successor-level capacity, and short-pilot evidence, and any macro must earn its place by a measured path-efficiency gain. \paragraph{Rings are constructed, not looked up.}
Ring topology is supported \emph{compositionally}: $\op{cycle\_close}$ inserts one legal bond between two nonbonded atoms and raises graph cycle rank by one, while $\op{cycle\_open}$ deletes one legal non-bridge cycle edge and lowers it by one. At the graph-topology level, every connected graph is a spanning tree plus its non-tree edges, so repeated close/open operations do not define support through a finite ring-template catalog. This graph statement does not prove a valence-valid path to every chemically labelled ring system within a finite edit budget; those claims require constructive executor replay and the topology tasks of experiment~D. The legacy whole-ring growth macro remains disabled. Ring-system restatement is retained provisionally for coordinated electronic changes, subject to the same reachability and capacity gates; whole-ring deletion is disabled in the bounded pilot. A future ring macro may be tested as an explicit efficiency proposal, never as the definition of ring support.

\subsection{Source processes and two sampling regimes}
\label{sec:sampling}

The network is source-agnostic (it reads only the current state and model time), so a source molecule is an initial condition, not a repeated network input. Editing and \emph{de novo} generation are nevertheless separate fitted regimes. The editing prior is trained from real-endpoint analogue programs and executor-generated support paths, begins from a supplied molecule, and is sampled as a \textbf{fixed-budget embedded molecular jump chain}: each step draws from the productive canonical-successor law, while continuous-time hazard magnitude is discarded. The unconditional lane requires a separate broad-organic checkpoint, a frozen de novo source distribution including the reversible null source, nonzero de novo training mass, and calibrated hazard. Its inference is a \textbf{timed CTMC}: evaluate $\lambda_\theta$, draw an exponential waiting time, sample a masked mark, commit, and recompute. The shared framework and executor do not make the editing checkpoint evidence for unconditional generation. For editing, the exact control object is indexed by the \emph{remaining budget} rather than by time (\cref{sec:control}).

\begin{proposition}[Closure and trans-dimensional reachability]\label{prop:closure}
Under \cref{ass:finite}, if $G_0\in\M$ then every state committed by the teacher or the learned process lies in $\M$ almost surely. Moreover, ignoring chemical labels, any two finite connected simple graphs are joined by a finite sequence of leaf births and deaths and non-bridge edge insertions and deletions with every intermediate connected; $\varnothing$ is joined reversibly to one-vertex graphs; and any cyclic topology is reached from an acyclic precursor by inserting its non-tree edges one at a time.
\end{proposition}

In words: committed states cannot leave the declared support, and the abstract graph basis is trans-dimensional and topology-changing. The null state is the sole non-molecular exception. The reachability half is a graph statement only: in \method every non-null step must additionally satisfy the molecular grammar, so a valence-feasible route between two \emph{particular} molecules is not implied, and it says nothing about finite-budget path cost. Dataset-pair reachability is therefore certified constructively by compiler replay (experiment~B) rather than inferred from \cref{prop:closure}. Proof in \cref{app:proofs}.

Both halves of \cref{prop:closure} are statements about \emph{states}. Making them true of the learned process, rather than of an arbitrary encoding of it, requires one more construction.

\section{The canonical molecular-successor quotient}
\label{sec:quotient-section}

Equations~\eqref{eq:quotient-rate} and \eqref{eq:productive-kernel} are definitions, but they are better read as decisions about the state-level object induced by the marked process. The successor fibers partition $\A(G)$, and four consequences follow. Aggregation removes symmetry and address multiplicity: deleting either of two equivalent methyls, or naming one Kekul\'e structure two ways, is one transition named twice, and without aggregation a molecule receives probability in proportion to how many ways the encoding can name it. It defines the pushed-forward process on molecular states rather than on the slot array of \cref{sec:transdim}; under the conditions below it makes controllers encoding-independent, since a controller sees only one scalar per successor; and it lets primitive and macro routes to one successor contribute to a single transition. The production editing kernel additionally conditions away canonical self and virtual events rather than counting them as fixed-budget edits. The completed diagnostic run optimized selected executable marks, except that graft already used a log-sum-exp over its canonical-successor aliases. Its molecular law is therefore the executor-and-canonicalization pushforward of the trained mark law; we do not retroactively describe that run as successor-trained. Equation~\eqref{eq:editing-v2-objective} is the declared replacement objective, but becomes a completed method claim only after its packed implementation and scaling gates pass.

It is tempting to say that any refinement or coarsening of marks preserving aggregate successor rates leaves the process and its controlled law unchanged. That is true only under two conditions the informal statement hides, and false for a practically important class of controllers.

\begin{definition}[Successor-preserving, rate-preserving re-encoding]\label{def:reencoding}
Two mark systems $(\A,\T,q)$ and $(\A',\T',q')$ on the same state space are so related if for every $G$ there is a surjection $\varphi_G:\A'(G)\to\A(G)$ with $\T_{\varphi_G(a')}G\simeq\T'_{a'}G$ for all $a'$, so marks may be split or merged only \emph{within} a successor fiber, never across fibers, and if $\sum_{a\in\Gset_H(G)}q(a\mid G,t)=\sum_{a'\in\Gset'_H(G)}q'(a'\mid G,t)$ for every $G,H,t$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Encoding invariance, and its exact scope]\label{prop:encoding}
Let two mark systems be related by a successor-preserving, rate-preserving re-encoding, under \cref{ass:finite}, and use the same state-level productive-commit predicate. Then \textup{(i)} the state-level generators coincide, hence so do the total hazards $\lambda_\theta=\lambda'_\theta$, the molecular CTMC laws, the productive embedded jump kernels $\Pmol=\Pmol'$ wherever defined, all time marginals, and the terminal law; \textup{(ii)} for any controller $\Psi_t$ that is \emph{fiber-constant} (a function of $(G,H,t,\omega)$ rather than of the mark), the controlled kernels of \eqref{eq:control-law} coincide, hence so do the controlled molecular laws; \textup{(iii)} hard support masks $\one\{C(H)\}$ and the finite-horizon Doob transform of \cref{thm:finite-doob} are fiber-constant by construction, so the values $h_b$ and the controlled kernels are identical under any such re-encoding.
\end{proposition}

In words: how the implementation names its edits is not part of the state-level model when the re-encoding preserves successors and aggregate rates. Two boundaries are load-bearing rather than pedantic. \emph{Fiber-crossing merges are ill-posed}, not merely lossy: merging marks with non-isomorphic successors would stop the executor being a function of the merged mark, which \cref{def:reencoding} excludes and the informal phrasing does not. And \emph{mark-level controllers are not generally invariant}: if $H$ is reachable by four symmetric aliases of rate $1/4$ under one encoding and by a single mark of rate $1$ under another, a mark-level power tilt $\Psi\propto q(a)^{\beta-1}$ gives $H$ controlled mass $4\cdot(1/4)^{\beta}$ versus $1$, differing for every $\beta\neq1$. Top-$k$ or nucleus truncation over marks and per-family tilts generally fail for the same reason; a per-family multiplier is invariant only if it is constant across every alias in each successor fiber. Our molecular controllers are written over successor states. The proposition concerns the pushed-forward process, not the training representation: mark-level supervision is generally not invariant to within-fiber refinement. \Cref{app:proofs} gives the proof and the full adversarial check; experiment~E tests it empirically.

Because the modelled object is a kernel on molecules, a controller can now be defined without reference to the rewrite vocabulary at all.

\section{Finite-horizon stochastic control of the molecular kernel}
\label{sec:control}

Let $C$ be a predicate on $\M$ and $\Psi_t(\cdot,\cdot;\omega):\M\times\M\to[0,\infty)$ a controller with parameters $\omega$. For the continuous-time construction, assume $\Psi_t$ is locally bounded on the registered finite horizon over base-supported transitions. Controlled generation is
\begin{equation}
\widetilde Q_t(G,H)=\one\{C(H)\}\;Q_{\theta,t}(G,H)\;\Psi_t(G,H;\omega),\qquad H\neq G,
\label{eq:control-law}
\end{equation}
with the diagonal re-summed to the negative total outgoing rate. One expression covers hard requirements, preference tilting, Doob control ($\Psi=h(H)/h(G)$), source-conditioned editing ($G_0=G_s$), mid-trajectory objective changes (time-varying $\omega_t$), and unguided generation ($C\equiv1,\Psi\equiv1$), with controllers swapped without retraining.

\begin{proposition}[Closure under control; pathwise invariance]\label{prop:control-closure}
Under \cref{ass:finite}, for any predicate $C$ and any nonnegative controller $\Psi_t$ satisfying the local-boundedness condition above, if $G_0\in\M$ then every state committed by \eqref{eq:control-law} lies in $\M$ almost surely, and the controlled support satisfies $\widetilde Q_t(G,\cdot)\ll Q_{\theta,t}(G,\cdot)$. If moreover $C\equiv1$ and $\Psi_t(G,H)>0$ for every base-supported $H\neq G$, the off-diagonal supports coincide. Consequently, for a predicate $P$ with $P(G_0)$ true, taking $C=P$ makes $P$ an invariant: $P(G_k)$ holds at \emph{every} committed $G_k$, not only at the horizon. If feasibility is evaluated before a separate downstream property oracle, that oracle need not be called on a state rejected by $P$.
\end{proposition}

In words: a controller may delete or reweight transitions but can never create one, so guidance cannot leave the valid molecules, and a rule-closed constraint becomes a property of the path rather than a filter on the endpoint. This is what makes \emph{pathwise} constraints expressible at all: the restriction $\A_P(G)=\{a:P(\T_aG)\}$ can be formed only because $\T_aG$ is a complete molecule on which $P$ is decidable.

\paragraph{Finite-horizon Doob control.}
Editing walks a fixed-$K$-step embedded chain in which the hazard and clock are discarded (\cref{sec:sampling}), so the exact control object is not the continuous-time generator transform: the desirability depends on the \emph{remaining budget}.

\begin{theorem}[Exact finite-horizon terminal tilt and exact continuation]\label{thm:finite-doob}
Let $\{P_{\theta,b}\}_{b=1}^{K}$ be row-stochastic transition kernels on a finite state space, indexed by remaining budget and including any explicitly declared absorbing terminal rows. A stationary embedded chain is the special case $P_{\theta,b}\equiv\Pmol$. Let $g\ge0$ be a nonzero terminal desirability and define $h_0=g$ and $h_b(x)=\sum_yP_{\theta,b}(x,y)h_{b-1}(y)$. At a state with $h_b(x)>0$ and $b$ steps remaining, let
\begin{equation}
P^{g}_{\theta,b}(x,y)=P_{\theta,b}(x,y)\,h_{b-1}(y)/h_b(x).
\label{eq:finite-doob}
\end{equation}
Then $P^{g}_{\theta,b}$ is row stochastic and its support is contained in that of $P_{\theta,b}$. Let $\Pr$ denote the uncontrolled law generated by the budget-indexed sequence. From $x_0$ with budget $K$ and $h_K(x_0)>0$,
\begin{equation}
\Pr\nolimits^{g}(X_K=y\mid x_0)=\Pr(X_K=y\mid x_0)\,\frac{g(y)}{h_K(x_0)},
\qquad h_K(x_0)=\textstyle\sum_y\Pr(X_K=y\mid x_0)g(y).
\label{eq:finite-tilt}
\end{equation}
Replacing $g$ by $g'$ at an intermediate state $x$ with $b'$ steps remaining and $h'_{b'}(x)>0$ yields exactly the $g'$-tilt of the continuation from $(x,b')$. A state with $h_b(x)=0$ is unreachable under the conditioned law, and the transformed kernel is undefined there rather than numerically patched. Support equality holds at a controlled row only when $h_{b-1}(y)>0$ for every base-supported successor $y$.
\end{theorem}

In words: with an exact desirability, a budget-conditioned reweighting of existing molecular transitions realizes the specified terminal distribution exactly, and changing the objective mid-trajectory is a fresh finite-horizon problem from the current molecule. The transform never creates a transition, but hard conditioning may prune base edges. Proof sketch: the $h$ factors telescope along every reachable path, so all paths to a fixed endpoint are reweighted by the same $g(y)/h_K(x_0)$; \cref{app:proofs} gives the argument and the continuation case.

\begin{remark}[Exactly where exactness stops]\label{rem:exact-scope}
Exactness is \emph{budget-specific}: the controlled marginal after fewer than $K$ steps is an $h_{K-j}$-tilt, not the terminal $g$-tilt, so early stopping does not inherit \eqref{eq:finite-tilt} and a budget-agnostic controller is not exact even with a perfect value. It requires an \emph{exact} $h$ and exact simulation; on the full molecular state space $h$ is intractable, the controller is learned, and it will be reported as an approximation with a calibration curve and a backward-equation residual. And the terminal form covers endpoint desirabilities: genuinely path-dependent rewards need a Feynman--Kac potential, a killed generator, or an augmented state.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The goal-conditioned controller}
\label{sec:goalcond}

Exactness is available only where $h$ can be computed. At scale we learn a budget-conditioned desirability
\begin{equation}
h_\phi\big(b,\,G;\,G_s,\,z,\,m\big),
\label{eq:goalcond}
\end{equation}
with $b$ the remaining edit budget, $G_s$ the source lead, $z$ a preference or target region, and $m$ a protected-substructure mask, and set $\Psi_t=h_\phi(b-1,H;\cdot)/h_\phi(b,G;\cdot)$ in \eqref{eq:control-law}. Three properties follow from earlier sections rather than from the controller's design. It is \emph{source-conditioned without a source-conditioned base}: $G_s$, $z$, $m$, $b$ enter only through $h_\phi$, so one base kernel serves every campaign. It is \emph{representation-independent}: $h_\phi$ scores molecular successors, so it is fiber-constant and \cref{prop:encoding} applies. And it \emph{retargets without restarting}: conditioning on $b$ makes a mid-trajectory change of $z$ the exact continuation problem of \cref{thm:finite-doob}.

Three lanes stay distinct. The \emph{exact} controller computes $h$ by backward recursion, exists only on enumerable spaces, and is the ground truth of experiment~F. The \emph{learned} controller \eqref{eq:goalcond} is an approximation that will be evaluated and calibrated as one. \emph{Reward fine-tuning}, changing $\theta$ so the base prefers high-reward regions, moves the reference process rather than reweighting it, forfeiting the guarantee that one base kernel serves all objectives; no result here depends on it.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

The contribution is a generative framework whose capabilities are properties of one object: rewriting supplies executable event semantics, Generator Matching learns how probability flows on them, aggregation turns marks into a molecular kernel, and control acts on that kernel. We do not claim to out-optimize strong task-level search: a graph genetic algorithm and PMO-style methods are strong optimizers \citep{jensen2019graphga,gao2022pmo}, and uniform legal rewriting over our own edit graph is a strong controller the learned rate must beat on the record. Graph validity is closure of the \emph{design space}, not chemistry in a flask: it implies nothing about synthesizability, strain, or oracle reliability, and a rewrite trajectory is not a synthesis route. Exactness is claimed only where $h$ is computable, at the declared horizon, for terminal desirabilities (\cref{rem:exact-scope}); the deployed controller is an approximation and labelled as one, while support preservation and pathwise invariance carry to scale because they are properties of the support, not of a value function.

Scope boundaries we chose: charge is carried rather than designed; stereochemistry and formal-charge changes are outside the current editing contract; birth has zero or one existing neighbour; and states contain at most $40$ active atoms. The bounded Active8 pilot disables both whole-ring growth and whole-ring deletion. Compositional cycle close/open defines graph-topology edits without a finite ring-template catalog, but this does not imply a valid finite-budget path to every chemically labelled ring system. The completed large-data editing run is a diagnostic baseline rather than a selected model: no checkpoint is frozen, ring opening and Graft require successor-level diagnosis, and the editing-V2 rebuild must pass staged capacity and retention gates. Experiment~A still requires a separate broad-organic \emph{de novo} checkpoint. Experiment~F is being rebuilt on the frozen \texttt{carbon\_6\_slots} verification graph, and no superseded exactness artifact is used as evidence (\cref{app:status}). Hard support guarantees imply nothing about FCD, likelihood optimality, mixing, or chemical usefulness.

\section{Conclusion}

We asked whether a generative process can be built out of executable rewrites rather than a generic corruption. Rewrite Generator Matching answers yes, and \method tests what that construction buys in chemistry: a trans-dimensional process that creates and destroys atoms and changes ring topology, whose every non-null committed state is a supported molecule, whose equivalent mark encodings collapse to one molecular kernel, and whose finite-horizon Doob transform is exact wherever the bounded kernel is enumerable. \method learns an executable stochastic rewrite process and turns its induced canonical molecular process into a substrate for exact and dynamic design.

\section{Extended related work}\label{app:related}

\paragraph{Generator Matching and jump processes.}
Generator Matching unifies flow, diffusion, and jump generative models by regressing a marginal generator from tractable conditional generators \citep{holderrieth2025generator}; latent-variable formulations extend it to processes with unobserved structure \citep{billera2026latent}. \rgm is a jump instantiation in which the mark alphabet is state-dependent, guarded, and executable, and in which several marks may map to one successor.

\paragraph{Discrete graph diffusion and flows.}
DiGress and related discrete diffusion models corrupt node and edge categories and learn a denoiser \citep{vignac2023digress,austin2021d3pm,campbell2022ctdd}; DeFoG formulates graph generation by discrete flow matching \citep{qin2025defog}; further variants explore score, spectral, and continuous-time formulations \citep{jo2022gdss,jo2023grum,xu2024disco,siraudin2024cometh}. Their intermediates are discrete graphs but need not be connected or valence-admissible, so an oracle query at an intermediate is not chemically defined.

\paragraph{Variable cardinality and trans-dimensional processes.}
Trans-dimensional generative modeling via jump diffusion learns processes that move between spaces of different dimension \citep{campbell2023jump}; this is the closest formal precedent for \eqref{eq:transdim}, and we claim no novelty for variable-dimension generation as such. The distinction is that our dimension changes are typed, guarded graph edits with application conditions, executed by the same executor that validates every other transition, rather than dimension jumps in an ambient continuous space. GrIDDD adds monotone node insertion and deletion to graph diffusion \citep{ninniri2025griddd}; Edit Flows builds insertion, deletion, and substitution flows for variable-length objects \citep{havasi2025editflows}; Morph changes cardinality of 3D molecular graphs by unbalanced optimal transport \citep{franke2026morph}; DDSBM learns CTMC bridges for graph transformation \citep{kim2025ddsbm}.

\paragraph{Grammars, constrained diffusion, and rewriting.}
Molecular hypergraph grammars make validity structural and improve data efficiency \citep{kajino2019mhg,guo2022grammar}; fragment and junction-tree models generate through a motif vocabulary \citep{jin2018jtvae}. We claim no novelty for graph grammars. ConStruct's projector is confined to edge-deletion-invariant topological properties (planarity, acyclicity, lobster structure, bounded degree), whereas a required scaffold or SMARTS motif is edge-\emph{insertion}-invariant, the opposite class \citep{madeira2024construct}; PRODIGY projects onto aggregate count and threshold sets and has no operator for a specific labeled subgraph \citep{sharma2024prodigy}; CoCoGraph fixes formula and degree sequence by double-edge swaps that rewire which atoms bond, so a designated scaffold is not representable \citep{ruizbotella2026cocograph}. Stochastic graph rewriting supplies the rule/match/application-condition ontology and CTMC semantics from contextual propensities \citep{danos2015thermodynamic,behr2021rewriting}; reaction-based GFlowNets traverse executable synthesis transformations \citep{koziarski2024rgfn,zhu2026spacegfn}.

\paragraph{Editors, optimizers, and controllers.}
MARS runs annealed MCMC over graph-fragment edits with a learned proposal; MIMOSA iteratively adds, replaces, and deletes under property and similarity constraints; a graph genetic algorithm remains a strong and sample-efficient optimizer, and PMO is the standard efficiency benchmark \citep{xie2021mars,fu2021mimosa,jensen2019graphga,gao2022pmo}. These methods couple edit proposals or transition rules to a task-conditioned target distribution or search objective. \method instead learns an objective-independent stochastic reference transport and applies swappable control at inference; we claim no novelty for valid-state molecular editing itself. Doob $h$-transforms are classical; inference-time controllers (rank-directional tilting, Feynman--Kac and SMC correctors, value guidance) are orthogonal to \rgm and appear here as instances of $\Psi$ in \eqref{eq:control-law}.

\paragraph{Declared scope.}
States carry at most $N_{\max}=\ScopeMaxAtoms{}$ active heavy atoms, and the heads emit \ScopeClasses{} neutral $(\text{element},\text{valence})$ classes over C/N/O/F/S/P/Cl/Br/I/B, a vocabulary admitting \LeadCoverageBroad{} of the benchmark lead molecules where a neutral C/N/O/F vocabulary admits \LeadCoverageCnof{}. Formal charge is an input but not a head dimension, so charge is \emph{carried} rather than designed; stereochemistry, isotopes, radicals, and hybridization are not encoded. \Cref{app:operators,app:model} give the full ontology, application conditions, charge policy, and scope fingerprint.

\paragraph{Graft.}
Cut a single bond whose removal would disconnect the graph, choose a reattachment endpoint, and add the new bond in the same atomic transaction, so no disconnected state is ever exposed; atom count and the entire relocated fragment are preserved. In the editing regime the fragment is restricted to a pendant subtree crossing one bond, so ring cores never travel. The successor is identified by the general canonical key, and one shared enumerator serves both the model's proposal mask and the teacher construction, which is what guarantees a supervised graft lies inside the mask it is scored against.

\paragraph{Compositional ring construction, in full.}
$\op{cycle\_close}$ inserts one bond between two nonbonded atoms with valence headroom; because every non-null source is connected, an admitted insertion raises graph cycle rank by one. $\op{cycle\_open}$ deletes one non-bridge cycle edge and lowers graph cycle rank by one. At the untyped graph level, every connected graph is a spanning tree plus its non-tree edges. This explains why primitive close/open operations do not define topology support through a finite ring-template dictionary. It does not prove that the production grammar admits a valence-valid path to every chemically typed ring system, nor that a useful path fits a finite editing budget. Those are executor-replay and experiment~D questions. Whole-ring growth and deletion are disabled in Active8. $\op{ring\_aromaticity\_restate}$ remains as one coordinated bond-order rewrite, pending its own capacity and reachability gates.

\paragraph{Why coordinated restatement is one rewrite event.}
Ring-system restatement has one executable successor and one learned event probability. Its payload specifies a coordinated set of bond-order changes that the executor validates as a transaction. Treating such a coupled electronic change as unrelated sequential proposals would expose order-dependent intermediate states and would change the stochastic object. This rationale does not extend automatically to whole-ring addition or deletion, which remain disabled and require separate support, cost, and learnability evidence before any future ablation.

\paragraph{Ring topology coverage, compositionally.}
Let $G$ be a connected untyped graph with spanning tree $T$. The non-tree edges $E(G)\setminus E(T)$ number $|E(G)|-|V(G)|+1$ and determine its graph cycle rank. Inserting those edges one at a time realizes $G$'s abstract cyclic topology while preserving connectivity, and removing them in the reverse order restores $T$. The molecular executor adds typed bond, valence, aromaticity, and support conditions, so the graph construction is a structural argument only. The admissible chemical subset, action-by-action replay success, and finite-budget reachability for monocyclic, fused, spiro, bridged, macrocyclic, and cage targets are measured rather than assumed in experiment~D.

\section{Source Priors and Couplings}\label{app:prior}

\subsection{Degree-bounded carbon-tree sampler}

Given size law $\mu$ on $\{1,\ldots,N_{\max}\}$:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item sample $N\sim\mu$;
    \item sample a Pr\"ufer sequence with rejection or capped construction so every degree is at most four;
    \item decode the tree, assign neutral carbon atom states and single bonds, and derive implicit hydrogens;
    \item assert connectivity, valence validity, and the declared slot count.
\end{enumerate}
The source sampler consumes only $\mu$ and an RNG seed. For a training pair, the tree draw is independent of the endpoint conditional on the selected source size.

\subsection{Stationary size jitter}

Let $J$ be a Markov kernel on sizes with stationary law $\mu$. Drawing $N_1\sim\mu$ then $N_0\sim J(\cdot\mid N_1)$ preserves the source marginal:
\begin{equation}
\Pr(N_0=m)=\sum_n\mu(n)J(m\mid n)=\mu(m).
\end{equation}
A local proposal $n\to n\pm1$ with a Metropolis--Hastings accept/reject correction is a simple reversible choice. The jitter scale controls teacher transport cost but must not be confused with the source law. At inference, $N_0$ is drawn directly from $\mu$ without sampling a target size.

\section{Program Compilation Algorithms}\label{app:compiler}

\subsection{Primitive connectivity-preserving transport}

For completeness, any source can be reduced topologically to one retained atom: delete non-tree edges to expose a spanning tree, then remove leaves. Reverse the construction for a target spanning tree, add chords, and apply typed restatements. The production compiler uses direct common structure and Grafts because the one-atom route is valid but statistically wasteful.

\subsection{Graft transport}

Given equal-order carbon tree $T_0$ and target $G_1$:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item align temporary source slots with target slots and choose a spanning tree $T_1\subseteq G_1$ maximizing $|E(T_0)\cap E(T_1)|$;
    \item while $T\neq T_1$, select $e\in E(T_1)\setminus E(T)$; adding $e$ creates a unique cycle, from which select $f\in E(T)\setminus E(T_1)$; execute the corresponding atomic subtree Graft and validate;
    \item restate retained atom states and tree-bond classes in a guard-feasible order;
    \item install target chords as cycle-closing bond insertions;
    \item canonicalize and assert exact equality with $G_1$; replay the complete program independently.
\end{enumerate}
The graph-level edge-exchange loop performs at most $(n-1)-|E(T_0)\cap E(T_1)|$ iterations. Chemistry guards may reject a nominal exchange, so the implementation searches alternative cycle edges and uses a constructive fallback. A compilation failure is explicit; it is never converted to a truncated target.

\subsection{Ring macros are optional proposals, not current support}\label{app:ring-catalog}

The bounded Active8 registry uses no whole-ring growth or deletion event. Ring topology is edited through primitive cycle close/open operations, while coordinated ring-system restatement handles admitted bond-order changes. A future ring macro would alter one-event reachability and path cost under a fixed budget, even if its lowering used only primitive operations. It must therefore be treated as a registered efficiency proposal with matched primitive-equivalent accounting, not assumed to preserve the finite-horizon process.

Any future macro catalog must be frozen before evaluation and audited for the observed transformations it covers, the tail it excludes, executor replay, canonical-successor aliasing, and capacity under matched compute. A finite-catalog comparator belongs in experiment~D only if it is legitimately runnable and support differences are reported. It does not define the current framework or rescue a failed primitive operator by assumption.

\subsection{Program-cache reproducibility}

Programs are stored as rewrite instructions plus byte-packed exact graph checkpoints rather than a dense graph at every progress position. A sampled state replays at most $c-1$ validated actions from the nearest checkpoint. CPU compilation writes atomic, signature-validated shards with a manifest containing split hashes, compiler version, chemistry grammar, operator-capability and support hashes, prior or coupling configuration, seeds, and checkpoint interval. A GPU training stage refuses to start from an incomplete cache. These choices change throughput and storage, not the mathematical path distribution.

\section{Proofs}\label{app:proofs}

\subsection{Proof of \cref{prop:closure} (closure half)}

Induct on committed events. The base state is in $\M$. If $G_n\in\M$, the CTMC has support only on $a\in\A(G_n)$; by \eqref{eq:legal-fiber}, $G_{n+1}=\T_aG_n\in\M$. Local boundedness excludes explosion on the finite horizon, so only finitely many jumps occur almost surely. The conclusion applies equally to teacher programs and learned sampling because both invoke the same executor. \qed

\subsection{Proof of \cref{prop:control-closure} (closure under control, and pathwise invariance)}

The controlled off-diagonal rate \eqref{eq:control-law} is a pointwise product of a nonnegative indicator, the learned rate, and a nonnegative controller, hence nonnegative, and it vanishes whenever $Q_{\theta,t}(G,H)=0$. By \eqref{eq:quotient-rate}, $Q_{\theta,t}(G,H)>0$ only if some $a\in\A(G)$ has $\T_aG\simeq H$, and then $H\in\M$ by \eqref{eq:legal-fiber}. So the controlled process has support contained in $\Sset(G)\subseteq\M$, and the closure induction above applies verbatim with $Q_{\theta,t}$ replaced by $\widetilde Q_t$. Local boundedness is preserved because the hypothesis gives a finite $\sup\Psi_t$ on base-supported transitions over the registered finite horizon, hence $\widetilde Q_t\le(\sup\Psi_t)Q_{\theta,t}$. If $C\equiv1$ and $\Psi_t(G,H)>0$ at every base-supported $H\neq G$, then $\widetilde Q_t(G,H)>0$ iff $Q_{\theta,t}(G,H)>0$ off the diagonal, so those supports coincide. Neither factor can make $\widetilde Q_t(G,H)>0$ where $Q_{\theta,t}(G,H)=0$, which is the assertion that no controller creates support. \qed

\paragraph{Pathwise invariance.}

For the second claim, let $\A_P(G)=\{a\in\A(G):P(\T_aG)\}$ be the restricted applicable set, i.e.\ \eqref{eq:control-law} with $C=P$ and $\Psi\equiv1$. Induct on committed events. $P(G_0)$ holds by hypothesis. If $P(G_k)$ holds, the controlled process commits only $a\in\A_P(G_k)$, and $P(\T_aG_k)=P(G_{k+1})$ holds by the definition of $\A_P$. Hence $P(G_k)$ for every committed $k$, almost surely. Feasibility is decided before commitment. When $P$ is separate from a downstream scoring oracle, rejected successors can be discarded before that oracle is called. \qed

\begin{remark}
The predicate must be evaluable on a complete molecule, which is exactly what the state space guarantees and what an ambient-space process does not: for an incomplete or possibly disconnected intermediate, ``will this trajectory end containing a specified labeled substructure'' is not decidable from the intermediate, so the restriction $\A_P$ cannot be formed.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Proof of \cref{prop:encoding} (encoding invariance)}

\emph{(i)} By \cref{def:reencoding} the fibers correspond: $\varphi_G$ maps $\Gset'_H(G)$ into $\Gset_H(G)$ for every $H$, and surjectivity plus successor preservation make $\{\Gset'_H(G)\}_H$ a partition of $\A'(G)$ refining nothing across fibers. Rate preservation then gives, for every $G,H,t$,
\[
Q'_{\theta,t}(G,H)=\!\!\sum_{a'\in\Gset'_H(G)}\!\!q'(a'\mid G,t)=\!\!\sum_{a\in\Gset_H(G)}\!\!q(a\mid G,t)=Q_{\theta,t}(G,H).
\]
Summing over $H$ yields $\lambda'_\theta(G,t)=\sum_H Q'_{\theta,t}(G,H)=\lambda_\theta(G,t)$, so the total hazards agree without any further hypothesis. This is the answer to the natural worry that a refinement, which must renormalize $p_\theta$ over a larger applicable set, could perturb the hazard: it cannot, because the hazard is the sum of the aggregates. The generator $(L_{\theta,t}f)(G)=\sum_{H\neq G}Q_{\theta,t}(G,H)[f(H)-f(G)]$ therefore agrees, and by uniqueness of the finite-state Kolmogorov forward solution under \cref{ass:finite} so do all time marginals and the terminal law. On every row with positive total hazard, $\Praw(H\mid G,t)=Q_{\theta,t}(G,H)/\lambda_\theta(G,t)$, so the raw pushforwards agree. Because the productive predicate is the same state-level predicate in both encodings, \eqref{eq:productive-kernel} then gives $\Pmol=\Pmol'$ on every row with positive productive mass; the same declared absorbing convention handles stalled rows. Thus the productive embedded jump chains agree as well. Note this is an identity of laws over \emph{state} trajectories; the two systems obviously induce different laws over \emph{mark} trajectories, which are over different alphabets.

\emph{(ii)} If $\Psi_t$ is fiber-constant then it is a function $\Psi_t(G,H;\omega)$, so
\begin{align*}
\widetilde Q'_t(G,H)&=\one\{C(H)\}\,Q'_{\theta,t}(G,H)\,\Psi_t(G,H;\omega)\\
&=\one\{C(H)\}\,Q_{\theta,t}(G,H)\,\Psi_t(G,H;\omega)\;=\;\widetilde Q_t(G,H),
\end{align*}
and the diagonal, being determined by the off-diagonal row, agrees too. The hard mask is fiber-constant by definition, since $C$ is a predicate on states.

\emph{(iii)} $h_b$ is defined by backward recursion from the productive kernel at each remaining budget, every one of which is invariant by \emph{(i)}, so $h_b=h'_b$ for every $b$ by induction. The two encodings therefore have the same reachable set $\{G:h_b(G)>0\}$, and on that set $\Psi_b(G,H)=h_{b-1}(H)/h_b(G)$ is invariant and fiber-constant. The controlled kernels of \cref{thm:finite-doob} agree wherever they are defined. \qed

\paragraph{Adversarial checks that the statement must survive, and one it does not.}
Four attack surfaces were tested before this proposition was stated.
\emph{(1) Hazard renormalization:} handled in \emph{(i)}, the hazard is the sum of the aggregates, so preserving all aggregates preserves it, and no extra hypothesis is needed.
\emph{(2) Merging marks with different legality masks:} if $a_1,a_2$ have the same successor but differ in legality at $G$, only the legal ones are in $\A(G)$, so the aggregate is over the legal subset and the hypothesis is stated on $\A(G)$; a merge is admissible exactly when the merged mark's legality reproduces that aggregate. What is \emph{not} admissible is a merge across successors, which would make $\T$ ill-defined on the merged system, and \cref{def:reencoding} excludes it, and the informal ``any refinement or coarsening'' phrasing does not, which is why the condition is stated.
\emph{(3) Composition with the Doob transform:} see \emph{(iii)}; it survives, because the value is a function of the invariant kernel.
\emph{(4) Refinements that change the applicable-set cardinality:} these are the generic case and are covered: $|\A'(G)|\neq|\A(G)|$ is expected under splitting, and nothing in the argument uses the cardinality, only the fiber sums.
\emph{The check it fails.} A controller that is not fiber-constant breaks \emph{(ii)}, and this is not a corner case. Let $\Gset_H(G)$ contain four symmetric aliases of rate $1/4$ under one encoding and one mark of rate $1$ under another, with all other successors identical. A mark-level power tilt $\Psi(a)\propto q(a)^{\beta-1}$ assigns $H$ controlled mass $4\cdot(1/4)^{\beta}$ versus $1$, which differ for every $\beta\neq1$. Top-$k$ or nucleus truncation over marks and per-family tilts therefore generally fail; a family multiplier is an exception exactly when it is constant across all aliases of every successor fiber it touches. Two further scope limits are recorded rather than hidden: the proposition is about the \emph{population} process, so it says nothing about gradients, variance, or sample efficiency; and the training \emph{objective} is invariant only when the teacher term is aggregated, since $r\log q_\theta(a)$ evaluated at one alias changes under splitting while $r\log\sum_{a\in\Gset_H}q_\theta(a)$ does not. This is precisely why the graft teacher in the historical run was scored by a log-sum-exp over its successor group and why \eqref{eq:editing-v2-objective} aggregates every teacher fiber.

\subsection{Proof of \cref{thm:finite-doob}}

Write $\Pr_x$ for the law of the uncontrolled budget-indexed chain started at $x$ and $h_b(x)=\E_x[g(X_b)]$, which satisfies $h_0=g$ and the stated backward recursion by conditioning on the first step. On a row with $h_b(x)>0$, \eqref{eq:finite-doob} is well defined and stochastic because $\sum_yP_{\theta,b}(x,y)h_{b-1}(y)=h_b(x)$. Its support is contained in the corresponding base row because multiplication by $h_{b-1}(y)$ cannot make a zero base probability positive. The two supports are equal exactly when every base-supported successor has positive $h_{b-1}$. If $h_b(x)=0$, no path from $x$ reaches a positive-desirability endpoint in $b$ steps, so $x$ is unreachable under the conditioned law and no transformed row is defined there.

For a path $x_0,\dots,x_K$, write
\[
\pi_K(x_{0:K})=\prod_{k=0}^{K-1}P_{\theta,K-k}(x_k,x_{k+1}).
\]
If $\pi_K(x_{0:K})>0$, then under the controlled chain, whose step from $x_k$ uses budget $b=K-k$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr\nolimits^{g}(X_{0:K}=x_{0:K})
&=\pi_K(x_{0:K})\prod_{k=0}^{K-1}
\frac{h_{K-k-1}(x_{k+1})}{h_{K-k}(x_k)}\\
&=\pi_K(x_{0:K})\frac{h_0(x_K)}{h_K(x_0)},
\end{aligned}
\]
because the $h$ factors telescope. Since $h_0=g$, every path to a fixed endpoint $y$ is reweighted by the same factor $g(y)/h_K(x_0)$, and summing over paths gives \eqref{eq:finite-tilt}. The normalizer is $h_K(x_0)=\E_{x_0}[g(X_K)]$, the value with the \emph{full} budget at the start.

For the continuation statement, apply the same argument to the chain started at $x$ with horizon $b'$ and terminal desirability $g'$: the telescoping is over $k=0,\dots,b'-1$ with $h'_{b'}$ as normalizer, so the endpoint law is the $g'$-tilt of $\Pr_x(X_{b'}=\cdot)$. Note this is in general \emph{not} the same as having used $g'$ from step $0$, which is why comparing continuation against restart is a meaningful experiment rather than a tautology.

Finally, the marginal at $j<K$ is $\Pr^{g}(X_j=y)=\Pr(X_j=y\mid x_0)\,h_{K-j}(y)/h_K(x_0)$, a $h_{K-j}$-tilt rather than a $g$-tilt; these coincide only when $j=K$. Hence early stopping does not inherit \eqref{eq:finite-tilt}, as stated in \cref{rem:exact-scope}. \qed

\subsection{Proof of \cref{prop:closure} (reachability half)}

For connected $G$, choose a spanning tree $T_G$. Delete every edge in $E(G)\setminus E(T_G)$; each is on a cycle at deletion and is therefore not a bridge, so connectivity is preserved. Repeatedly delete leaves until one vertex remains. From one vertex, reverse leaf insertions to construct a spanning tree $T_H$ of $H$, then insert edges in $E(H)\setminus E(T_H)$. Every intermediate is connected.

For aligned trees $T$ and $T_1$, take $e\in E(T_1)\setminus E(T)$. Adding $e$ creates a unique cycle. That cycle contains an edge $f\in E(T)\setminus E(T_1)$; otherwise $T_1$ would contain a cycle. Replacing $f$ by $e$ is one subtree Graft, preserves the tree property, and increases overlap with $T_1$ by one. Repetition terminates after at most $(n-1)-|E(T_0)\cap E(T_1)|$ exchanges. \qed

\subsection{Proof of \cref{prop:gm} (population minimizer)}

Fix occupied $(G,t,H)$ and let $Y=R_t^Z(H\mid X_t,K_t)$ conditional on $X_t=G$. The part of \eqref{eq:gm-objective} depending on scalar prediction $u\ge0$ is
\begin{equation}
\ell(u)=u-\E[Y\mid X_t=G]\log u,
\end{equation}
with target-only terms omitted. For $u>0$, $\ell'(u)=1-\E[Y\mid X_t=G]/u$ and $\ell''(u)=\E[Y\mid X_t=G]/u^2\ge0$. If the mean is positive, the unique minimizer is its value; if zero, the infimum is attained at $u=0$ by continuity. Finite sums commute with conditional expectation, so the result applies after alias aggregation. \qed

\subsection{Proof of \cref{prop:gm} (endpoint law)}

The program mixture induces marginal law $p_t$ satisfying the Kolmogorov forward equation with the marginal generator $Q_t$ of \cref{sec:rgm}; this is the standard conditional-to-marginal Generator Matching identity. Under \cref{ass:finite}, the finite-state forward equation has a unique solution. If $Q_{\theta,t}=Q_t$ and both laws begin at $\pzero$, their marginals agree for all $t$. By \cref{ass:finite}, the program mixture terminates at $\pdata$, hence so does the exact learned process. \qed

\subsection{Importance-correct progress stratification}

Let $p(k\mid t,Z)$ be the binomial progress law and $h(k\mid Z)$ a family-balanced proposal. Sample from $\widetilde p=(1-\rho)p+\rho h$ and weight by $w=p/\widetilde p$. For any integrable per-progress loss $g$, $\E_{\widetilde p}[wg]=\sum_kp(k)g(k)=\E_p[g]$. Since $\widetilde p\ge(1-\rho)p$, support is retained for $\rho<1$.

\section{Model, Training, and Sampling Details}\label{app:model}

\subsection{Neural architecture}

The graph encoder embeds atom type, formal charge, implicit hydrogen count, bond class, model time, and ring/topology context. Message passing produces node and graph representations. A graph head predicts total hazard where the timed process requires it; masked family and operand heads score complete legal marks. Canonical-successor training sums the probability of all marks that execute to the teacher molecule, rather than rewarding one arbitrary alias. The current candidate backbone has hidden width $256$ and $6$ message-passing layers, but the released architecture, parameter count, optimizer, learning-rate schedule, precision, batch size, throughput, and peak memory remain \XXX{} until the T1 and P50 gates freeze them. Scratch initialization is allowed. If a warm start is used, semantically matching parameters are transferred by label and inherited capabilities receive explicit parity and retention probes; no de novo checkpoint is required by the framework.

\subsection{Training estimator}

For each minibatch item:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item draw or load $(G_0,G_1,Z)$ from the declared coupling and compiled cache;
    \item sample time $t$ and progress $k$, optionally from the importance-correct stratified proposal;
    \item reconstruct $X_t=G_k^Z$ from its nearest packed checkpoint;
    \item encode $X_t$ once and form analytic legality masks;
    \item evaluate the objective bound to the run contract: \eqref{eq:editing-v2-objective} for the productive editing-V2 lane, a calibrated rate objective for the timed \emph{de novo} lane, or \eqref{eq:gm-objective} only in an implementation that actually parameterizes its aggregated rate terms;
    \item aggregate every alias of the teacher successor before the editing identity loss and update $\theta$.
\end{enumerate}
We log hazard separately from productive canonical-successor NLL, teacher-successor probability and rank, raw mark count, canonical successor count, alias multiplicity, effective update mass, family/operand diagnostics, illegal-mask disagreements, and gradient norm. Model selection uses the frozen validation-only canonical-successor criterion and safeguards, never test FCD or the already inspected historical test aggregate.

\subsection{Piecewise-constant timed and embedded-chain samplers}\label{app:sampler}

\begin{figure}[tb]
\centering
\fbox{\begin{minipage}{0.94\linewidth}
\textbf{(a) Timed CTMC (\emph{de novo}).} Sample $G\sim\pzero$, set operational time $s=0$. Under the registered piecewise-constant time schedule, hold rates fixed between time-grid boundaries. While $s<S$ and the event budget is not exhausted: (i)~predict the total legal hazard and masked mark probabilities; (ii)~draw an exponential waiting time; (iii)~if it crosses the next boundary, advance to that boundary, otherwise sample one legal marked rewrite, execute $G\leftarrow\T_aG$, and advance by the waiting time.

\vspace{3pt}
\textbf{(b) Embedded jump chain (editing).} Sample $G=G_s$ (the lead), set the remaining budget $b=K$. While $b>0$ and $Z_\theta^+(G,t)>0$: sample a productive successor $H\sim\Pmol(\cdot\mid G,t)$ under the successor-level controller of \cref{sec:control}; then sample an executable alias $a\in\Gset_H(G)$ from $p_\theta(a\mid G,t)/\Praw(H\mid G,t)$ and execute $G\leftarrow\T_aG\simeq H$; decrement $b$. Return the current molecule, or the archive of all visited molecules.
\end{minipage}}
\caption{The two inference regimes share a rate-law factorization and executor but use separately fitted checkpoints. The de novo timed CTMC uses the hazard for event timing; source-conditioned editing uses the productive canonical-successor jump chain at a fixed edit budget.}
\label{alg:sampling}
\end{figure}


Let $0=s_0<\cdots<s_J=S$ be the operational-time grid and define the registered de novo rate model to be piecewise constant in operational time on every interval $[s_j,s_{j+1})$ (while still recomputing after a state change). Starting from $G\sim\pzero$, at grid interval $j$:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item compute $\lambda=\lambda_\theta(G,s)$ and masked marked probabilities;
    \item if $\lambda=0$, advance to $s_{j+1}$ and log a stall;
    \item otherwise draw $\Delta\sim\mathrm{Exponential}(\lambda)$;
    \item if $s+\Delta\ge s_{j+1}$, set $s=s_{j+1}$ without an event;
    \item else sample $a$ hierarchically, execute $G\leftarrow\T_aG$, set $s\leftarrow s+\Delta$, and recompute all rates.
\end{enumerate}
This is exact Gillespie simulation of the declared piecewise-constant time-inhomogeneous CTMC. If instead the network is interpreted as varying continuously with time, left-boundary freezing is an approximation, not an exact ancestral sampler, and grid-refinement convergence and endpoint sensitivity must be reported. The event budget is a numerical safeguard and its hit rate is reported. There is no rejection loop over invalid actions: analytic masks and the executor define zero support, and any disagreement is an auditable implementation error.

\subsection{Complexity}

Let $n\le N_{\max}$, $d$ be hidden width, $L$ message-passing layers, and $C_r(G)$ the tensorized candidate count for family $r$. One rate evaluation costs the graph encoder plus masked heads, approximately $O(L(n d^2+|E|d)+\sum_r C_r(G)d)$ for the dense implementation, rather than one graph encoding per canonical successor. Compilation is offline and program-dependent; Graft tree alignment is polynomial and each edge exchange is validated. Final measured preprocessing time, peak memory, train throughput, and sample throughput are \ComposeTrainHours, \ComposePeakMemory, \XXX, and \ComposeThroughput.

\section{Failure Modes, Broader Impact, and Reproducibility}

\paragraph{Failure modes.}

Downstream property guidance requires access control, dual-use review, toxicity assessment, and wet-lab governance. Corpus bias and cheminformatics blind spots remain even when formal validity is guaranteed.
